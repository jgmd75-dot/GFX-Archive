Dynamics

CH81

A modular channel strip built from the GFX range: pick an engine for each slot and mix and match.

Overview

CH81 is a full channel strip in the 81 series: input, gate, compressor, equaliser, saturation and output in one compact panel. Each slot has three or four engines to choose from, and every engine is the sound engine of a GFX plug-in itself - set the same controls and it sounds like the original. Pick, for example, Tony Q into Fet81 into the Gate, or Board EQ into the OB Q compressor into the expander, and swap any of them while the music plays.

The signal runs: INPUT (gain, low cut, high cut) > GATE > EQ and COMP in the ORDER you choose > SAT > OUTPUT (level and the house output stage).

  • GATE - GATE (the GFX Gate), EXPANDER (the OB Q console expander) or DRUM GATE (a fast keyed gate new to this strip).
  • COMP - FET81, OB Q or SMOOTH.
  • EQ - TONY Q (Tony Q 550, TKY and NYC), BOARD (Board EQ) or SKYE.
  • SAT - IRON or TRANS (an output transformer or a transistor line stage), HEAD (the tape head of Headsmoother) or TS-7.

Each engine keeps its own settings, so you can set up two compressors, say, and flip between them to compare. Only the chosen engine in each slot runs, so the strip costs no more CPU than the plug-ins you picked.

Reach for it for

  • A complete channel on any track, without building a chain of plug-ins
  • Trying a different compressor or EQ in the same place in seconds
  • Vocals, drums, bass and guitars from one familiar panel
  • Coloured console-style processing with one supply that breathes across the whole strip

Two themes

CH81 has two panel themes, the TKY / NYC keys in the header, named after the two cities of the Tony Q 550: TKY, charcoal with vermillion keys (the default), and NYC, a blue-grey console with amber keys. The theme changes only the look - the labels are in English in both, and every engine can be picked in every slot whichever theme is showing. Presets leave the theme as it is.

Engine keys and IN keys

Each slot has a row of lit keys: the lit one is the engine in the slot. Changing an engine fades the strip out, swaps the engine at the silent point and fades back in (about 30 ms, plus the new engine's latency), so it never clicks. The engine you switch to starts from a clean state, with the settings you last gave it.

The small grey IN button at the right of each slot's title (its amber LED is lit while the slot is in) switches the slot in or out with a 10 ms crossfade. Out, the slot's engine keeps running, and the bypass is delayed to match it, so the timing never jumps.

INPUT

LOW CUT
A 12 dB/octave high-pass, 20 - 500 Hz. Fully left is OFF. It glides to a new setting and fades in and out, so moving it never clicks.
HIGH CUT
A 12 dB/octave low-pass, 2 - 22 kHz. Fully right is OFF.
GAIN
The input level, -24 to +24 dB, before everything else. Click the knob (its LED, upper right) for GUARD: on by default, it eases very hot sources down towards about -18 dBFS average and catches fast peaks above -6 dBFS. It never boosts quiet sources.
IN meters
The level after GAIN and GUARD, left and right.

GATE

THRESH / RANGE
-60 to 0 dB; 0 - 80 dB.

EQ

In every EQ engine the knob caps follow the bands: LF brown, LMF blue, HMF green and HF red.

OUTPUT

Meters
The output, left and right, on LED bars from -48 to 0 dBFS. The bar shows the average level with VU ballistics (0 VU = -18 dBFS, where green turns amber); the single segment above it is the peak, held for a moment.
ORDER
One key: each press swaps EQ > COMP and COMP > EQ, and its legend shows the order in use. Its LED lights while the key is held. Changing it fades the strip out and back in.
LEVEL
The strip's output level, -24 to +12 dB.
DRIFT
The supply, 0 - 100% (50% by default) - see Drift below.
BAND SAT
The house output stage's band saturation, 0 - 100% (off by default): only the lows and highs are saturated.
CLIP
The house soft clipper is always on, at -0.3 dBFS; the lamp lights while it works.
latency
The strip's latency in samples, when it has any: the sum of the engines in use (Fet81 23, TS-7 31 or 36, GATE its look-ahead, DRUM GATE 1 ms). REAPER compensates for it.

Parameter list only

BIAS, LOW and HIGH bands and CEILING of the output stage. Every engine's controls are in REAPER's parameter list too, named after their slot and engine (EQ Board: HMF gain, Comp Fet81: Input ...).

Drift

One supply feeds the whole strip. Its voltage wanders slowly, and it sags under load (a slow follower of the input level), and every stage's headroom follows it: the saturation, the compressors' thresholds and the engines' own non-linear stages all move a little together, most of all when the strip is pushed. The engines' own drift is off; the strip breathes from the one supply. 50% is the standard amount, 100% twice that, 0% a perfectly steady supply.

Presets

Presets set the engines and their settings. The theme, GUARD, DRIFT and the output stage stay as they are (Reset all to defaults sets GUARD, DRIFT and the output stage too). A preset changes at the silent point of a short fade, so it never clicks, and every preset is level matched to Init.

Tips

  • To compare engines fairly, match their levels first (each engine's OUTPUT or the strip's LEVEL), then switch while the track loops.
  • COMP > EQ lets you boost after the compressor without it reacting to the boost; EQ > COMP makes the compressor hear your EQ.
  • Gate first is deliberate: the compressor then never brings up the noise the gate has just removed.
  • HEAD's BOOST and the output stage's BAND SAT add up: turn BAND SAT down if HEAD gets too fizzy.
  • Latency only changes when you change an engine (or the look-ahead or oversampling), and then at the silent point of the fade.
