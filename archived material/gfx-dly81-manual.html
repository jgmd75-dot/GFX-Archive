DLY81

The 81 series digital delays: eight models of the 1980s in two racks, European and Japanese.

Overview

DLY81 holds the 81 series' digital delays: eight models in two racks, one for each region. The Japanese rack has four Japanese delays of the 1980s: VINTAGE DIGITAL, a dual-phase chorus delay; PITCH 12, a 12-bit unit whose repeats climb or fall; COMPACT, a dark 8 kHz loop with compander breathing; and SAMPLE HOLD, which locks a loop. The European rack has four European studio and broadcast delays: KRISTALL (CRYSTAL), an ultra-clean dual delay with cross-feed; DYNAMISCH (DYNAMIC), a ducking delay whose repeats bloom when you stop; MULTI-TAP, up to eight chorused taps; and ZEITDEHNUNG (TIME STRETCH), with a pitch shifter in the loop.

Each rack has its own panel, its own model keys and its own presets. The two share the settings: TIME, SYNC, FEEDBACK, cross-feed, the filters, DRIVE, modulation, ducking, the vintage clock and bits, WIDTH, DRIFT and the input and output. TAPS belongs to the European rack and HOLD to the Japanese rack.

Reach for it for

  • Wide 80s chorus-delay on guitars, synths and vocals (Japanese rack)
  • Cascading octave and fifth repeats (PITCH 12)
  • Stutters and live phrase loops (SAMPLE HOLD)
  • Clean slapback, ping-pong and tempo-synced echoes (European rack)
  • Vocal echoes that stay out of the way while the singer sings (DUCK)
  • Rhythmic multi-tap patterns and shimmering pitch-shifted repeats

Two regions

DLY81 has one REGION switch, the EU / JPN keys at the top right of the panel. Each region is its own rack, with its own panel, its own four models and its own presets. Switching fades the sound out, changes over at the silent point and fades back in, so it never clicks. Switching starts the other rack clean. Old projects load in the rack they were saved with.

EU
The European rack: brushed aluminium with a cream band and dark blue print, pale blue displays, lit push buttons and two VU meters. CRYSTAL, DYNAMIC, MULTI-TAP and TIME STRETCH: clean, precise repeats from transformer-coupled studio and broadcast units.
JPN
The Japanese rack: a midnight blue casing and a dark grey panel with orange segment readouts, green LEDs, red status lights and rubber knobs. VINTAGE DIGITAL, PITCH 12, COMPACT and SAMPLE HOLD: modulated loops, pitch in the feedback, compander breathing and gritty 10 - 16-bit converters.

Controls

The header

EU / JPN
The region: the European or the Japanese rack, with its own panel and its own four models (see Two regions).
S / M / L
Panel size: small, medium or large. The panel also scales to fit the plugin window.
?

The Japanese rack

Programs and status keys

VINTAGE DIG
The rack chorus-delay. The LFO runs sine on the left and cosine on the right, so MOD DEPTH spreads the repeats wide.
PITCH 12
Every repeat is pitch-shifted by PITCH semitones, so with feedback the echoes climb or fall. This program always uses the vintage converters, at 32 kHz and the BITS setting (16 by default).
COMPACT
The compact unit: the loop is held under 8 kHz (HI CUT can go no higher), and a 2:1 compander squeezes the signal into the loop and expands it coming out, so the repeats breathe, with a faint hiss.
SAMPLE HOLD
The sampling delay. With HOLD on, the last TIME L of audio plays round and round; nothing new is recorded.
SYNC
Locks TIME L and TIME R to the project tempo as note values: 1/1 to 1/32, with dotted (D) and triplet (T) values.
HOLD
Freezes the loop in SAMPLE HOLD. Switching it on from another program selects SAMPLE HOLD. The display shows HOLD in red while it is held.
VINT
The vintage clock: the delay line runs at the old hardware's sample rate with nearest-sample reads, and the converters use BITS bits. Off is the clean MODERN path. Always lit in PITCH 12.

Display, meters and presets

DELAY L / DELAY R
The delay times in milliseconds, in orange. The green line underneath shows FREE RUNNING, or the SYNC note values and tempo, and the converter setting (VINTAGE with its bits, or MODERN 24-BIT).
INPUT / DELAY
Level meters for the input and for the delayed signal.
BITS
Steps the vintage converters through 10, 12, 14 and 16 bits (16 by default). Fewer bits give more grit and noise. Used when VINT is on (and always in PITCH 12).
PRESETS
Opens the list of factory presets. The ◄ / ► keys beside it step through the ten presets, and the display beside them shows the number of the one in use. The last entry resets everything to the defaults.

The knobs

Each knob has a green readout underneath that shows its value while you touch it, and a red light that is lit whenever the knob is away from its default. Double-click a knob to reset it; hold Shift while dragging for fine moves.

TIME L / TIME R
The left and right delay times, 1 to 2700 ms (375 and 500 ms by default), or a note value with SYNC on. Changing the time glides the pitch, as on the hardware.
FEEDBACK
How much of the delay is fed back for more repeats, 0 to 99% (35% by default).
CROSS
Cross-feed, 0 to 100%: sends the feedback to the opposite side, so the repeats ping-pong.
LO CUT
Thins the repeats below this frequency, 20 to 1000 Hz (80 Hz by default). Each repeat gets thinner.
HI CUT
Darkens the repeats above this frequency, 1 to 20 kHz (9 kHz by default). Each repeat gets darker.
MOD DEPTH
How far the LFO sweeps the delay time, 0 to 10 ms (0 by default). Low settings give a gentle shimmer, high settings a deep chorus or vibrato.
MOD RATE
The LFO speed, 0.05 to 10 Hz (0.8 Hz by default).
PITCH
The interval each repeat is shifted by in PITCH 12, from -12 to +12 semitones (0 by default). Labelled (PITCH) in the other programs, where it does nothing.
DUCK
Pulls the repeats down while you play, up to 18 dB, and lets them bloom back when you stop, 0 to 100%. Keeps busy parts clear.
RECOVERY
How quickly the repeats come back after ducking, 20 to 2000 ms (400 ms by default).
DRIVE
Drives the input stage before the delay, 0 to 100%, for a little grit.
WIDTH
The stereo width of the repeats, 0 to 150% (100% by default). 0 is mono; above 100% widens beyond the original.
MIX
Dry / delayed balance, 0 to 100% (30% by default).
LEVEL
Output level, -24 to +12 dB.

Drift

DRIFT
The clock is not quite steady: the left and right delay times wander slowly and independently, by up to a fraction of a millisecond, for a gentle, living pitch movement. 0 to 100% (50% by default): 50% is the standard amount, 100% twice that, 0 none; higher settings also wander a little faster.

IN / OUT

TRIM
Input level before anything else, from -24 to +12 dB.
GUARD
Off by default. When on, it gently eases very hot sources down towards about -18 dBFS average and catches fast peaks above -6 dBFS, so loud sources never overdrive the plugin. It never boosts quiet sources. An orange light shows while it is working.
SAT
Band saturation on the way out: only the lows and highs are saturated (the mids stay clean), with a soft curve that loosens as the level rises.

Presets (Japanese rack)

The European rack

Working the displays

Every parameter has its own display. Drag up or down on a display (Shift for fine moves), use the mouse wheel over it, or press the - / + keys underneath (hold for repeat). Double-click a display to reset it to its default. The last display you touched is outlined. Displays that do nothing on the current model are dimmed.

DELAY PROCESSOR

INPUT / ECHO meters
VU meters showing the input level and the level of the echoes (0 VU = -18 dBFS).
CRYSTAL
The ultra-clean dual delay: feedback filters, cross-feed for ping-pong, and a crossfade when the time changes, so the pitch never bends.
DYNAMIC
The same line with the ducker working. Choosing it sets DUCK DEPTH to 60% if it was at 0.
MULTI-TAP
Up to 8 taps (TAPS) spread evenly across the longer of TIME L and TIME R, alternating left and right, each quieter than the last and gently chorused by MOD DEPTH and MOD RATE. The feedback comes from the end of the line.
TIME STRETCH
The repeats pass through a pitch shifter (PITCH, -12 to +12 semitones per repeat) and a soft limiter inside the loop, so they can rise or fall forever without running away.
SYNC
Locks TIME L and TIME R to the project tempo; the displays then show note values (1/1 down to 1/32, with dotted D and triplet T values) and the header shows the tempo.
VINTAGE
Runs the delay line through 16-bit converters at the era's clock rate: 48 kHz on CRYSTAL, 32 kHz on DYNAMIC and MULTI-TAP, 40 kHz on TIME STRETCH.
PRESETS
Opens the list of factory presets.
◄ / ►
Step to the previous or next preset (wrapping round).
Preset display
Shows the last preset recalled (P1 - P10, or P--) and whether the times are FREE or SYNC.

TIME

TIME L / TIME R
The left and right delay times, 1 to 2700 ms (375 and 500 ms by default), or note values when SYNC is on (1/8D and 1/4 by default).

FEEDBACK

FEEDBACK
How much of the echo goes back into the line, 0 to 99% (35% by default). More gives more repeats.
CROSS-FEED
How much of each side's feedback crosses to the other side, 0 to 100%. Turn it up for ping-pong echoes.
LO CUT
A high-pass filter on the repeats, 20 to 1000 Hz (80 Hz by default). Each repeat gets thinner.
HI CUT
A low-pass filter on the repeats, 1 to 20 kHz (9 kHz by default). Each repeat gets darker.

DUCK

DEPTH
How far the echoes are pulled down while you play, 0 to 100% (up to about 18 dB). Works on every model.
RECOVERY
How long the echoes take to bloom back when you stop, 20 to 2000 ms (400 ms by default).

MODULATION

MOD DEPTH
How far each tap's time is swept, 0 to 10 ms. MULTI-TAP only.
MOD RATE
The speed of that sweep, 0.05 to 10 Hz. MULTI-TAP only.
PITCH
The pitch shift applied to each repeat, -12 to +12 semitones. TIME STRETCH only.
TAPS
The number of taps, 1 to 8 (6 by default). MULTI-TAP only.

CHARACTER

DRIVE
The input transformer, 0 to 100% (15% by default). The lows saturate first, for warmth and weight before the delay.
WIDTH
The stereo width of the echoes, 0 to 150%. 0 is mono; above 100 is wider than normal.
DRIFT
How much the delay clock wanders, 0 to 100% (50% by default). See Drift below.
MIX
The dry / echo balance, 0 to 100% (30% by default).

Drift

The delay clock is not quite steady: the left and right times each wander slowly and independently by a fraction of a millisecond, which keeps the repeats from sounding sterile. At 50% you get the standard amount; 100% doubles it and makes it wander a little faster; 0% switches it off.

IN / OUT

TRIM
Input level before anything else, -24 to +12 dB.
LEVEL
The output level, -24 to +12 dB.
GUARD
Off by default. When on, it gently eases very hot sources down towards about -18 dBFS average and catches fast peaks above -6 dBFS, so loud sources never overdrive the plugin. It never boosts quiet sources. GUARD CATCHING shows while it is working.
SAT
Band saturation on the way out, 0 to 100%: only the lows and highs are saturated (the mids stay clean), with a soft curve that loosens as the level rises.

Tips

  • For live phrase sampling, choose SAMPLE HOLD, set TIME L to the length you want to catch (or use SYNC), then press HOLD at the end of the phrase.
  • Automate TIME for tape-style pitch swoops: the time glides, so the repeats bend as it changes.
  • Add DUCK on vocals and lead lines so the echoes stay out of the way while the part is playing and fill the gaps afterwards.
  • For vocals, try DUCK DEPTH at 50 - 70%: the echoes stay out of the way of the words and fill the gaps.
  • Lower HI CUT and raise LO CUT for repeats that sit behind the mix rather than on top of it.
