Echo 34

Three bucket-brigade echoes in one: a stereo studio delay line, a small, hissy mini echo box and a stretching delay pedal.

Overview

Echo 34 is three bucket-brigade (BBD) echoes in one unit. The STUDIO / MINI / SQUASH keys in the header choose the model, and the faceplate changes with it. Each model keeps its own controls and settings, and only the selected one runs. A model change is click-free: the new model fades in while the old one's input closes and its repeats fade away over a quarter of a second.

STUDIO (navy face) is a stereo analogue delay line in the spirit of the budget analogue echo units of the late 70s and 80s. The input goes through a driven op-amp preamp into the delay; every repeat passes back through the same drive and the chip's clock filter, so the echoes get darker and grittier as they fade. LONG gives 50 - 550 ms, SHORT 50 - 110 ms for slapback and doubling.

MINI (painted black steel) is a small, cheap solid-state echo box in the spirit of the early-80s mini echoes: one BBD chip, a compander round it so quiet passages breathe and the hiss sinks between notes, and a narrow echo path (about 4.2 kHz at the shortest delays, 2.6 kHz at the longest). The echo is mono, as on the original; the dry signal stays in stereo. Short settings sound hard and metallic, longer ones give slapback or dark, deep repeats.

SQUASH (matte black and yellow) is a delay pedal in the spirit of the classic bucket-brigade analogue delays. GAIN drives the signal into the delay line through a warm, slightly lopsided overdrive; the dry signal stays clean. The tone filter sits inside the feedback loop, so every repeat comes back a little darker than the last, and long delay times darken a touch more, as a slower clock does on the real thing.

REGEN goes past 100%. There the loop starts to howl into steady self-oscillation, but a soft limiter inside it holds the level, so it never runs away. Turning TIME glides the delay rather than jumping, so the repeats bend in pitch as you move it.

The STRETCH key sweeps the delay time towards a second time, STRETCH TIME, at a speed set by RATE, then glides back when you let go. Everything in the delay line bends in pitch as it goes: a fast setting sounds like a tape being yanked, a slow one like a lazy dive. HOLD makes the key work while you hold it down; LATCH makes it click on and off.

In all three models, turning the delay time glides the delay rather than jumping, so the repeats bend in pitch while you turn it, and the repeats can be pushed into runaway self-oscillation.

Reach for it for

  • Warm, dark analogue echoes on guitars, vocals and synths (STUDIO)
  • Stereo doubling (STUDIO, DUAL CHIP)
  • Rockabilly slapback and quick ADT doubles (MINI)
  • Metallic short echoes and lo-fi dub repeats (MINI)
  • Dub throws with ECHO and MOMENTARY (MINI)
  • Warm, dark analogue repeats behind a guitar or synth lead (SQUASH)
  • Rockabilly slapback and short doubling (SQUASH)
  • Dub throws and runaway, howling feedback (SQUASH)
  • Pitch-bending tape-yank and dive effects on the echoes (SQUASH)

STUDIO

PREAMP

TRIM
Input level, -24 to +12 dB.
DRIVE
The preamp's drive, 0 to 100% (30% by default). It saturates the input unevenly (the two halves of the wave clip differently, adding even harmonics) and also sits in the feedback loop, so each repeat is driven again.

DELAY

LONG / SHORT
The chip. LONG: 50 to 550 ms. SHORT: 50 to 110 ms, for slapback and doubling; TIME stays within that range.
TIME
The delay time. Changes glide smoothly, so the repeats bend in pitch as you turn it.
REPEATS
Feedback, 0 to 110% (40% by default). Above 100% the repeats grow instead of fading - self-oscillation.
DUAL CHIP
Runs the right side about 3% longer than the left, so the two sides' repeats fall slightly apart: a wider, doubled stereo echo.
OSC LIMIT
On by default. Holds the feedback in check so self-oscillation stays at a usable level instead of running away without limit.

TONE

CLOCK LPF
The low-pass filter on the repeats, 1 to 8 kHz (3.5 kHz by default). Because it is inside the loop, every repeat is darker than the one before. Lower it for murky, distant echoes; raise it for brighter ones.
NOISE
The chip's clock noise added to the repeats, 0 to 100% (15% by default).

MASTER (STUDIO)

MIX
The dry / echo balance, 0 to 100% (35% by default). Up to 50% the dry signal stays at full level and the echo comes up; from 50% the dry fades out, leaving only the echo at 100%.
DRIFT
See Drift below.
OUTPUT
The output level, -24 to +12 dB.
OUT meters
Output level, left and right. Click them to reset the peak hold.

Drift (STUDIO)

DRIFT (0 - 100%, 50% by default): the chip's clock wanders, so the delay time breathes a little, and the clock filter - the tone of the repeats - slowly opens and closes. 50% is the standard amount, 100% twice that, 0 steady.

MINI

INPUT

INST / MIC
The two input sockets. INST is flat. MIC adds 12 dB of preamp gain, cuts the lows below about 150 Hz and brings up the hiss.
VOLUME
Input level into the preamp, -24 to +18 dB. Set it so the PEAK light only flickers; push it further and the preamp clips, roughly and a little unevenly.
PEAK
Lights as the preamp starts to clip.
ECHO
Echo on or off (on by default). Off stops new signal reaching the echo, and the repeats already in it die away naturally.
MOMENTARY
Does not latch: it works only while you hold it. With ECHO on, holding it pauses the echo; with ECHO off, holding it turns the echo on until you let go. It can be automated as a parameter.

ECHO

BALANCE
Dry to echo, 0 - 100% (35% by default). Up to 50% the dry signal stays at full level and the echo comes up; from 50% the dry fades out, leaving only the echo at 100%.
REPEAT
Feedback, 0 - 110% (35% by default). Past about 85% (2 o'clock) the repeats build up instead of fading; the loop clips like the original's op-amp, so it stays bounded.
REPEAT light
Glows when the repeats in the loop are strong.
DELAY TIME
20 - 400 ms (180 ms by default). The longer the delay, the darker and grittier the echo. With SYNC on, the readout shows the synced time.
SYNC
Click for a list: FREE, or a note value locked to the project tempo (1/32, 1/16T, 1/16, 1/8T, 1/16D, 1/8, 1/4T, 1/8D). The time stays within 20 - 400 ms.

MASTER (MINI)

DRIFT
See Drift below.
OUTPUT
The output level, -24 to +12 dB.
OUT meters
Output level, left and right.

Drift (MINI)

DRIFT (0 - 100%, 50% by default) works on two things. The chip's clock wanders, so the delay time breathes a little. And the supply voltage wanders and sags: the preamp's headroom and gain move slowly, and loud passages pull the supply down so the preamp squashes a little more. 50% is the standard amount, 100% twice that, 0 steady.

Neither model has an input guard or an output stage: the levels are set with TRIM / VOLUME and OUTPUT.

SQUASH

PEDAL

GAIN
Drive into the delay line, 0 to 100% (25% by default), up to about 24 dB of drive. A warm, slightly lopsided overdrive that adds even harmonics; the lows are trimmed before it so it never flubs, and the top is softened so it never fizzes. Only the repeats are driven - the dry signal stays clean.
TIME (yellow cap)
The delay time, 30 - 1500 ms (350 ms by default). With SYNC on it becomes a note value from 4 bars down to 1/32, including dotted (D) and triplet (T) values (1/4 by default). Turning it glides the delay, so the repeats bend in pitch rather than clicking. All times are limited to 4 seconds.
REGEN
Feedback, 0 to 130% (35% by default): how many repeats you hear. Past 100% the loop oscillates on its own and keeps going after the input stops; the limiter in the loop holds it at a steady level.
TONE
The high-frequency roll-off inside the loop, about 700 Hz - 9 kHz (0 to 100%; 55%, about 2.9 kHz, by default). The knob shows the frequency. Because it is inside the loop, each repeat passes through it again and gets darker.
LEVEL
The volume of the repeats, -24 to +6 dB (0 dB by default).
MIX
Dry / wet balance, 0 to 100% (35% by default). At 50% both are at full level; below that the repeats fade down, above it the dry signal does.

STRETCH

The settings for the STRETCH key.

TIME
The delay time the stretch sweeps towards, 30 - 2000 ms (900 ms by default). Longer than the main TIME bends the pitch down as it goes; shorter bends it up. The sweep follows an S-shaped curve, so the bend eases in and out.
RATE
How fast the stretch sweeps, 0 to 100% (50% by default): from a slow 4-second dive at 0% to a 30 ms yank at 100%. The same speed is used on the way back.
HOLD / LATCH
How the STRETCH key works. HOLD (default): the stretch lasts while you hold the key down and glides back when you let go. LATCH: click the key once to stretch and again to glide back.

The readout and lights

Time readout
The actual delay time in ms, including any stretch and drift, so you can watch it glide.
SYNC
Switches TIME between milliseconds (off, by default) and a note value at the project tempo (on).
TEMPO
A red light that blinks once for every repeat.
Yellow double dot
Lights while the stretch is on or still gliding back.
HOWL
Lights when REGEN is above 100% and the loop is sounding: a warning that it is self-oscillating.
STRETCH
The big lit key. Hold it (HOLD) or click it on and off (LATCH) to sweep the delay to STRETCH TIME; it lights while the stretch is on. It is also a parameter (Stretch), so you can automate it in REAPER.

MASTER (SQUASH)

DRIFT
0 to 100% (50% by default). See Drift below.
OUTPUT
Output level, -24 to +12 dB.
OUT meter
Left and right output levels.

Drift (SQUASH)

DRIFT makes the delay clock breathe: a slow, small wander of the delay time - a subtle wow, a little different on the left and right, which widens the repeats - and a gentle wander in the repeats' tone. 50% is the standard amount, 100% twice that, and the wander speeds up a little as you turn it up. At 0% the delay is steady.

Tips

  • For dub throws, use MINI with ECHO off and hold MOMENTARY to catch single words; ride DELAY TIME by hand for pitch swoops.
  • On a send, set MIX (STUDIO) or BALANCE (MINI) to 100% so only the echo comes back.
  • If STUDIO's echoes get too dark too fast, raise CLOCK LPF rather than REPEATS; if MINI's get muddy, shorten the delay.
  • Use STUDIO's SHORT chip with DUAL CHIP for a quick stereo double on a mono vocal or guitar.
  • Try the stretch while the repeats are still sounding: it bends what is already in the line, so the trick is in the timing.
  • A STRETCH TIME longer than TIME drops the pitch; shorter raises it. Set RATE low for a slow dive, high for a sudden yank.
  • Use LATCH when you want to automate the stretch or play it hands-free; HOLD is quicker for one-off throws.
