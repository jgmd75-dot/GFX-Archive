Reed 200

A sample-free reed electric piano, modelled from the hammer up, with its little speakers or a line out, the classroom tremolo and an FX row.

Overview

Reed 200 is a reed electric piano in the spirit of the classic 70s classroom and stage reed pianos, built on the Mark 73 engine. There are no samples: every note is built from the mechanism. A felt hammer flies at the key velocity and strikes a steel reed with a little solder weight at its tip. The reed rings with its own set of partials - a strong fundamental and a few very short high partials that give the attack its edge - and its sustain is shorter than a tine's, falling away faster up the keyboard.

The pickup is electrostatic: each reed swings in the slot of a charged plate, and the output follows the change of the capacitance between them. That curve has a broad, flat top and steep sides, so a soft note stays round and a hard note swings past the plate's edges and folds over: the reed piano's famous bark. Where the reed rests in the slot (VOICING) and how close the plate is (GAP) set how clean or gritty it is, and how early the bark comes in.

The sound goes through either the built-in pair of small speakers (narrow, nasal and quick to break up when pushed) or the line out, with the classroom tremolo, then the FX row: CHORUS, VIBE, DELAY and SPACE. DRIFT makes every sounding reed wander slightly out of tune on its own, as old reeds do.

Reach for it for

  • Reed piano for soul, pop, rock and 70s singer-songwriter parts
  • Barking, driven keys that respond to how hard you play
  • The classroom tremolo on chords and ballads
  • Lo-fi, dusty keys with drifting reeds
  • A softer, rounder reed tone through the line out

Loading and playing it

  1. Insert a new track and add Reed 200 to it. It is an instrument: it makes its own sound from MIDI and needs no audio input.
  2. Arm the track for recording, set its input to your MIDI keyboard and switch record monitoring on (or draw notes in a MIDI item).
  3. Play. The MIDI light in the header flashes on each note. A new instance opens at the large size on the Init preset.
  4. Use a sustain pedal: CC64 holds the dampers off, and the undamped reeds ring along with each other a little, as on the real instrument.
  5. To play it from one MIDI channel only, click OMNI in the header and choose the channel.

Reed 200 plays note on and note off with velocity, the sustain pedal (CC64) and all notes off (CC120 and CC123, which fade every note out over 25 ms rather than cutting it dead). A key struck again while it is still ringing is not a new voice: the hammer meets the moving reed, as on the real thing. There is no pitch bend: like the instrument it is modelled on, the reeds have a fixed pitch, and pitch-bend messages are ignored. MIDI is not passed on down the chain.

HAMMER & REEDS

HARDNESS
The hammer felt, 0 - 100% (50% by default). A soft hammer stays on the reed longer and only excites its low partials: round and mellow. A hard one bounces off quickly: a brighter, sharper attack.
DECAY
How long held notes ring, 0 - 100% (50% by default): from about a third to nearly three times the natural decay, long in the bass and short at the top.
RELEASE
How quickly the dampers stop a note when you let go, 0 - 100% (40% by default): from about 30 ms to about 1.4 s.

PICKUP

VOICING
Where the reed rests in the pickup's slot, 0 - 100% (50% by default), from CLEAN to BARK. Towards CLEAN the reed sits on the steep side of the curve: a round fundamental with a little odd colour, and the bark only on the hardest notes. Towards BARK it rests nearer the middle of the plate: more even harmonics and a nasal, gritty tone, and hard notes fold over into bark sooner. The level is kept even.
GAP
How far the plate sits from the reed, 0 - 100% (50% by default), from NEAR to FAR. Near, the swing is large against the plate and notes break up sooner; far, they stay cleaner and more even across the dynamics.
Pickup window
Shows the capacitance across the plate (the white curve), the plate, and where the reed rests (the red line and dot), which moves with VOICING. While a note sounds, a shaded band shows how far the latest note's reed is swinging (SWING); with nothing playing it reads AT REST.

AMP

SPEAKERS / LINE
SPEAKERS (the default) is the built-in pair of small oval speakers in an open cabinet: a narrow band from about 120 Hz to 5.5 kHz, a nasal lift around 1.2 kHz and a small power amp that breaks up when pushed. LINE is the preamp out: full range and cleaner. The switch is made under a brief fade.
TREBLE
A treble shelf around 3 kHz, -12 to +12 dB (0 dB by default).
OVERDRIVE
How hard the amp is driven, 0 - 100% (0% by default): from a warm thickening to a broken-up growl.

TREMOLO

DEPTH
The classroom tremolo, 0 - 100% (off by default). As on the original, a lamp and a photocell ride the preamp's level, so the pulse is smooth and rounded. The red light glows with the lamp.
RATE
The tremolo speed, 0.1 - 12 Hz (5.6 Hz by default, about the original's fixed rate).

PLAYING

VELOCITY
How much your key velocity changes the hammer speed, 0 - 100% (70% by default). At 0% every note is struck at the same speed; low settings give a light curve; 100% is a long hammer throw, with the widest range from a soft touch to a slammed, barking note. The tone changes with the speed, not only the level.
WIDTH
Spreads the keys across the stereo field, low notes to the left and high notes to the right, 0 - 100% (30% by default). 0% is mono.
POLYPHONY
How many notes can sound at once, 4 - 32 (16 by default). When they are all in use, the oldest note (released ones first) is faded out quickly to make room. Fewer voices use less CPU.

TUNING

FINE
Fine tuning, -50 to +50 cents.
RANDOM
A small random tuning offset on every new note, 0 - 10 cents (0 by default): from a freshly tuned instrument to a tired, out-of-tune one.

MASTER

VOLUME
The output level, -24 to +6 dB. A soft knee on the bus above -6 dBFS keeps the peaks under -1 dBFS before the output stage.
DRIFT
How much the instrument drifts, 0 - 100% (15% by default). See Drift below.
MECH
The mechanism noises, 0 - 100% (30% by default): the hammer thump on the key bed and its click, the tick of the short high partials on hard hits, the dampers landing and the knock as a key comes up. At 0 you hear only the reeds.
SAT
Band saturation on the way out, 0 - 100% (0% by default): only the lows and highs are saturated (the mids stay clean), with a soft curve that loosens as the level rises.
OUTPUT CLIP
A soft clipper on the output (always on) catches overs at the ceiling.
Meters
Output level, left and right.

CHORUS, VIBE, DELAY and SPACE

The FX row, after the amp and in that order. Each effect has its own lit ON key and fades in and out smoothly; all four are off on Init.

CHORUS: RATE / DEPTH
A stereo bucket-brigade-style chorus, the left and right sweeps a quarter cycle apart. RATE 0.05 - 8 Hz (0.6 Hz by default), DEPTH 0 - 100% (50% by default).
VIBE: RATE / DEPTH
A four-stage photocell phaser, its lamp sweeping the notches: the swirl of the classic lamp-driven pedals. RATE 0.1 - 10 Hz (1.2 Hz by default), DEPTH 0 - 100% (50% by default); DEPTH sets both how wide the sweep is and how much of it you hear.
DELAY: TIME / FEEDBACK / MIX
A ping-pong delay: the echoes start on the left and bounce across. The feedback is warm and saturating, losing top and bottom on each repeat. TIME 20 - 1500 ms (375 ms by default), FEEDBACK 0 - 95% (35%), MIX 0 - 100% (25%).
SYNC
Click to step through SYNC: MS (TIME in milliseconds), 1/4, 1/8 DOT (dotted), 1/8 and 1/4 TRIP (triplet). The note values follow the project tempo, up to 1.5 s; while synced, the TIME knob is greyed and shows the synced time.
SPACE: ROOM / PLATE / GATED
The reverb type. ROOM: a room of about 0.5 - 1.3 s. PLATE: bright and long, about 1.4 - 4 s. GATED: the 80s gated reverb, a long, bright tail cut short about 0.3 s after each note.
AMOUNT
The amount of reverb, 0 - 100% (25% by default). For ROOM and PLATE it also lengthens the tail.

Drift

DRIFT is reed tuning wander. Every sounding reed wanders slowly and on its own, a few cents at most (more as you turn DRIFT up), so chords beat and shimmer gently the way an old reed piano does; the whole action's tuning wanders a little too, every note lands a fraction of a cent off, and the plate's alignment breathes. At 0% the instrument stays exactly where you set it.

In REAPER's parameter list only

Bias / Sat low band / Sat high band / Ceiling
The rest of the output stage: the saturation's even-harmonic bias, where its low and high bands start (40 - 400 Hz and 2 - 16 kHz) and the clip ceiling (-0.3 dB by default).

Knobs: drag up or down (Shift for fine moves); double-click or Ctrl-click to reset a knob to its default.

Presets

A preset sets every sound control, the FX row, DRIFT and VOLUME. SAT, the output stage and the MIDI channel are left as they are.

Init
Every control at its default: the middle voicing through the SPEAKERS, no tremolo and no effects.
Classroom
A little softer and cleaner, a touch of tremolo and a small room.
Soft Felt
A soft hammer, clean voicing, the plate further away and the top rolled off: round and quiet.
Bark & Grit
A hard hammer, voicing towards BARK, the plate near and a wide velocity range, with some drive.
Tremolo Classic
The classic deep tremolo at 5.6 Hz.
Gospel Bark
Barky voicing pushed through a driven amp, with a little tremolo and a room.
Mellow Line
The LINE out with a longer decay, a softer hammer and chorus.
Lo-Fi Dusty
Dark and barky, with tremolo, random detune, lots of drift and more mechanism noise.
Bright Pop
A harder hammer and +4 dB of treble through the LINE out, with a plate.
Ballad Tremolo
A long, soft ballad sound with a slow tremolo and a plate.
Drive Amp
The little amp cranked, with a short echo.
Chorus Keys
The LINE out with a wide chorus and a room.
Echo Room
A dotted-eighth echo and a room, with a little tremolo.
Dark Room
A soft hammer, clean voicing, the plate far away and the top rolled off: deep and warm.

Tips

  • Play with dynamics: with VELOCITY high, soft notes stay round and hard notes bark.
  • VOICING is the biggest single change to the character: try it before reaching for TREBLE.
  • If the bark comes in too early, move GAP towards FAR; for more bark with less effort, move it towards NEAR.
  • SPEAKERS is the classic sound on its own; use LINE when you want the reeds full range, for example under a mix with its own amp or reverb.
  • Turn DRIFT up for the slightly out-of-tune shimmer of an old instrument, or down to 0 for a freshly tuned one.
