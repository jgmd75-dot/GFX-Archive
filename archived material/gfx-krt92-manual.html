Mastering

KRT92

Series 92's studio enhancer: a valve harmonic exciter with a bass contour, or a late-90s DSP multiband maximiser, on one switch.

Overview

KRT92 is the Series 92 counterpart of KRT74: the "enhancer" box in the rack of a 90s home studio, with two models on one switch.

ANALOG is a valve harmonic exciter. The band above TUNE (1.5 - 8 kHz) is driven through a valve at a steady level, and only the new harmonics are mixed back in with AMOUNT, so the top gains sparkle and presence without simply getting louder. BASS is a bass contour below 100 Hz: the lows are rounded by a valve, lifted, and their sub rumble pulled back, so the bottom is tighter and warmer. WARMTH adds a little valve warmth over the whole signal. DIGITAL is a late-90s DSP multiband maximiser, with no added harmonics and a 20-bit output: three bands, with upward expansion on the highs above TUNE for clarity, a transient shaper on the lows below 120 Hz for punch, and ALIGN, which delays the low band slightly so the highs arrive first.

The processing runs at 2x oversampling. AMOUNT, TUNE, BASS and WARMTH / ALIGN are level matched in both models, and the two models are matched to each other, so the controls and the switch change the character, not the loudness. Changing the model or LISTEN fades the sound out and back in; the latency is 31 samples, reported to REAPER.

Reach for it for

  • Air and presence on dull vocals, acoustic guitars and samples
  • A tighter, warmer bottom on bass and kick
  • Clarity and punch on a mix or drum bus, with no added harmonics
  • Lifting dull tape transfers and old samples

Getting started

  1. Insert KRT92 on a track or bus. It opens on Init: ANALOG, AMOUNT at 40%, TUNE at 3.5 kHz, BASS at 30%.
  2. Press SOLO under LISTEN and set TUNE so you hear only the part of the top you want to brighten; press it again to hear the whole signal.
  3. Turn AMOUNT for more sparkle and BASS for a tighter, warmer bottom; the level stays put.
  4. Switch to DIGITAL for clean clarity and punch: AMOUNT lifts the highs, PUNCH the low-band attacks, ALIGN lets the highs lead.
  5. Blend with the MIX fader and set the level with the OUTPUT fader.

INPUT

IN / OUT ladders
LED ladders, -30 to +6 (0 = 0 VU): the level going into the enhancer and coming out.
TRIM
Trim before everything, -24 to +12 dB (0 dB).
GUARD
The house input guard: eases very hot sources down (on by default). Its LED lights while it works.

VALVE EXCITER / HF ENHANCER

The high section, in red (the house HF colour). Its LCD shows TUNE; its IN / OUT keys glide the section in and out.

TUNE
The bottom of the high band, 1.5 - 8 kHz (3.5 kHz). Lower brings in presence, higher only air.
AMOUNT
ANALOG: how much of the valve's harmonics is mixed back in, 0 - 100% (40%). The band is driven at a steady level, so the colour is the same at any input level. DIGITAL: the upward expansion of the band above TUNE - a lift that grows on the band's transients - for clarity and definition.
LISTEN
SOLO plays only what the section adds: ANALOG the harmonics, DIGITAL the enhanced high band. Use it to set TUNE. The change fades out and back in.
IN / OUT
The high section in or out (in by default), gliding with no click.

BASS CONTOUR / LOW PUNCH

The low section, in brown (the house LF colour); its IN / OUT keys glide the section in and out.

BASS / PUNCH
ANALOG: the bass contour, 0 - 100% (30%): the lows below 100 Hz are rounded by a valve and lifted, and the sub rumble below about 35 Hz is pulled back - tighter and warmer, with a little scoop of the low mids. DIGITAL: a transient shaper on the band below 120 Hz lifts the attack of each kick and bass note.
IN / OUT
The low section in or out (in by default), gliding with no click.

OUTPUT

WARMTH / ALIGN
ANALOG: a little valve warmth over the whole signal, 0 - 100% (30%). DIGITAL: phase alignment - the low band is delayed by up to 1.5 ms so the mids and highs arrive first.
DRIFT
The series' drift, 0 - 100% (50% is the standard amount): on ANALOG the valves warm up and the mains wander, so their bias and the gain drift slowly and the channels never quite match; on DIGITAL only a trace of gain drift remains.
SAT
The house output stage's band saturation (off by default). Its soft CLIP is always on; the red LED lights when it catches a peak.
MIX
A fader with a blue cap: dry to processed, 0 - 100% (100%). The dry signal is delayed to match.
OUTPUT
A fader: the output level, -24 to +12 dB (0 dB).

Two models

Every Series 92 unit has two models, chosen with the ANALOG / DIGITAL keys in the header - the two halves of a serious 90s home studio. ANALOG is the boutique outboard: valve, opto and transformer colour. DIGITAL is the first affordable 16 / 20-bit converters and DSP: clean, precise and a little glassy. The two are level matched; switching fades the sound out and back in. The panel lamps glow amber on ANALOG and green on DIGITAL.

ANALOG
A valve harmonic exciter: the valve's harmonics above TUNE, a valve bass contour below 100 Hz and a little valve warmth - sparkle, a tighter, warmer bottom.
DIGITAL
A late-90s DSP multiband maximiser: upward expansion of the highs, a low-band transient shaper and phase alignment, no added harmonics, a 20-bit output.

Presets

A preset sets AMOUNT, TUNE, BASS, WARMTH / ALIGN, the two sections' IN / OUT, MIX, OUTPUT and the model, and turns LISTEN off; TRIM, GUARD, DRIFT and the output stage are left as they are. The presets are levelled to play at a similar loudness.

Tips

  • Set TUNE with LISTEN on: you hear only what the high section adds, so it is easy to find the band that needs it.
  • Switch between ANALOG and DIGITAL on the same settings to hear harmonics against clean expansion: the level stays the same.
  • A little goes a long way on a mix: AMOUNT at 20 - 40% is usually enough; use Parallel Excite's MIX for more.
  • On DIGITAL, ALIGN is subtle on its own; listen to the kick and bass against the cymbals as you turn it.
