Overland

A go-anywhere drive and weight processor with the mood of an 80s four-wheel-drive camper: three transfer ranges, lift, terrain, diff lock, fog, and paint that has a sound.

A Lab plug-in. The Lab is where new GFX plug-ins are built in the open: they may change sound or controls between versions.

Overview

Overland is a drive for adding density, warmth and weight, from a touch of thickening to a full-on off-road grind. The signal goes through LIFT (the mud trimmed below about 150 Hz), then the drive, then TERRAIN's grit, LIFT's top shelf and FOG, then DIFF LOCK and the livery's stereo width, TERRAIN's wobble and a finishing stage, and out through the house output stage.

DRIVE works two layers at once. One is the TRANSFER curve you pick (2H, 4H or 3L). The other is a tape-style stage. As DRIVE rises the blend moves from the TRANSFER curve towards the tape stage, and the curve is driven less as the tape takes over: you get tape density rather than ever more fuzz. Both layers are level-matched at -12 dBFS, so DRIVE changes the character, not the loudness.

The paint is part of the sound. Each of the eight liveries has its own voicing: darker paint is warmer and denser (the lows saturate harder, the highs lift less, the image narrows slightly, and the top rolls off as DRIVE rises); lighter paint is brighter and wider (more air, and the image widens with a slight left / right detune as DRIVE rises). A finishing stage follows the paint too: a weighty limiter and mid clipper on the warm liveries, a clean peak catcher on the middle one, an airy soft knee on the bright ones. Livery changes glide.

The output stage's clipper is always on, with its ceiling at -0.3 dBFS. Latency is 1.5 ms (reported to REAPER). There is no DRIFT dial; TERRAIN supplies the movement.

Reach for it for

  • Warmth and thickness on a mix bus or stem
  • Heavy, glued low end on bass and kick (3L)
  • Dense, controlled drive on drums without mush (4H)
  • A dusty, wobbly, lo-fi tape feel (TERRAIN, FOG)
  • Brightening and widening a dull stereo source (a light livery, LIFT)

Controls

The header

PRESET
Click the word PRESET for the preset list (with "Reset all to defaults" at the end).
◀ / ▶
Step to the previous or next preset, wrapping round.
Preset display
Shows the preset's name. Click it for the preset list too.

The window and the louvres

Spectrum
The rainbow bars in the foreground of the dusk scene are a live spectrum of the output, 24 bands from 40 Hz (red) to 16 kHz (violet).
Louvres
The two slatted vents are the left and right output meters, 3 dB per slat; the top slat lights at about -3 dBFS, and the top three slats turn yellow.

The controls

DRIVE
0 to 10, default 4. How hard it is driven, and how far the blend moves from the TRANSFER curve towards the tape stage. Loudness stays roughly put.
LIFT
0 to 10, default 3. Ground clearance: trims the mud below about 150 Hz before the drive (up to 8 dB) and adds a high shelf at 6 kHz after it (a few dB; more on light liveries, less on dark ones).
TERRAIN
0 to 10, default 2. Tarmac to rough track: a slow wobble in pitch and level. Above half way it also roughens the transients of the top end (above about 3 kHz).
TRANSFER
Click 2H, 4H or 3L (the change crossfades). 2H: one gentle stage on the whole signal, for warmth and thickening. 4H: four bands (split at 150 Hz, 1.2 kHz and 5 kHz), each driven on its own, then summed: density without mush. 3L: low range for hill climbs: the lows (below about 200 Hz) are driven harder with slow, heavy glue and a little sub-octave that grows with DRIVE; the top is driven lightly.
DIFF LOCK
Everything below about 120 Hz to mono: a solid, centred low end.
FOG
A warm haze: a soft low-pass at 6.5 kHz.
OUTPUT
-12 to +12 dB, before the output clipper.
4WD / 2WD
Click the plate. 4WD (default): every saturation stage is anti-aliased, for a clean top end. 2WD: everything runs plain, lighter on the CPU, with a rawer top and a little aliasing fizz. The switch dips the output for about 5 ms so it never clicks.
Livery chips
Eight paint chips: click one to repaint the panel and change the voicing. The line under them reads LIVERY - BRIGHT / WIDE, BALANCED or WARM / DENSE. Toy Teal, Sage, Desert Tan, Alpine Blue and Alpine Cream are bright and wide; Midnight Gold is balanced; Sunset Bronze and Bottle Green are warm and dense.

Knobs: drag up or down; Ctrl-click returns to the default. Values show while you hover.

Tips

  • Try the same settings in a light and a dark livery: the paint changes the tone as much as an EQ would.
  • On a full mix, 4H keeps the drive from smearing the low end into the top; 3L is for bass and kick.
  • DIFF LOCK is worth leaving on for bass-heavy stereo material, especially with 3L's sub-octave.
  • Keep TERRAIN at 5 or below for movement without grit; above 5 the top end starts to roughen.
  • 2WD is a quick way to save CPU on many instances, or to get a rawer, fizzier top on purpose.
