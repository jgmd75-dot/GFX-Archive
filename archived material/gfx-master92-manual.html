Mastering

Master92

Series 92's mastering processor for CD: a valve EQ, an opto compressor and a valve-and-iron limiter, or a 90s digital mastering box with a 3-band compressor, a brickwall limiter and 16-bit dither, on one switch.

Overview

Master92 is the Series 92 mastering processor: mastering for CD in a serious 90s home studio. The signal runs through EQ, a COMPRESSOR, STEREO width and mono bass, a LIMITER and, on DIGITAL, the WORD LENGTH stage that takes the master down to 16 bits for CD. Every section has its own IN / OUT keys, and there are meters all the way.

ANALOG is a valve mastering chain: a valve program EQ with broad, sweet curves into a valve stage, an opto bus compressor that is slow and gluey and lets go more slowly the longer it works, with a valve make-up amp, and a valve-and-iron soft limiter that rounds what it lets through. VALVE sets how hard the valves and the iron work. DIGITAL is the 90s digital mastering processor: clean digital EQ, a 3-band compressor with fixed crossovers, a look-ahead brickwall limiter with a dBFS ceiling, and the word length: 16 (or 20) bits with TPDF dither and a choice of noise shaping, the signature of the era.

The two models are level matched at the default settings. The latency is the same in both, and with the limiter out (2 ms less one sample, 95 samples at 48 kHz, reported to REAPER).

Reach for it for

  • Finishing a stereo mix for CD or any 16-bit release
  • Valve warmth and opto glue on the master bus
  • Band-by-band control of a mix with the 3-band compressor
  • A brickwall ceiling and properly dithered 16-bit output

Getting started

  1. Insert Master92 on the master bus. It opens on Init: ANALOG, 4:1 at -10 dB, a ceiling of -0.3 dBFS.
  2. Set THRESHOLD until the GR ladder lights 1 - 3 dB on the loud parts; add MAKEUP to taste.
  3. Shape the tone with LOW, MID and HIGH, and use MONO BASS to keep the low end centred.
  4. Push DRIVE into the limiter for loudness, watching its GR ladder and the LUFS read-out.
  5. Switch to DIGITAL for the 90s digital chain; leave WORD LENGTH in at 16 bits for a CD master.

INPUT

IN ladder
An LED ladder, -30 to +6 (0 = 0 VU): the level coming in.
TRIM
Trim before everything, -24 to +12 dB (0 dB).
GUARD
The house input guard: eases very hot sources down (on by default). Its LED lights while it works. On a hot master you may want it off.

EQ

The IN / OUT keys in the title strip switch the EQ in or out (the change fades out and back in). The title reads VALVE on ANALOG and DIGITAL on DIGITAL. Each band's gain and FREQ knobs share its colour: LOW brown, MID green, HIGH red.

LOW
A low shelf, -6 to +6 dB (0 dB). Its FREQ knob below sets the corner, 30 - 300 Hz (80 Hz).
MID
A bell, -6 to +6 dB (0 dB). FREQ 200 Hz - 8 kHz (1.5 kHz). Very broad on ANALOG, tighter on DIGITAL.
HIGH
A high shelf, -6 to +6 dB (0 dB). FREQ 2 - 20 kHz (10 kHz): above about 14 kHz it is an air band.
ANALOG / DIGITAL
On ANALOG broad, gentle shelves and bell, then a valve stage that adds warmth (with VALVE); on DIGITAL clean Butterworth shelves and no colour. A band at 0 dB is out of the path.

COMPRESSOR

The IN / OUT keys in the title strip switch the compressor (and MAKEUP) in or out. The title reads OPTO on ANALOG and 3-BAND on DIGITAL.

GR ladders
The gain reduction, 0 to -20 dB, lit from the right. ANALOG shows one ladder; DIGITAL one for each band (LO, MID, HI).
THRESHOLD
-30 to 0 dB (-10 dB).
MAKEUP
A fader with a yellow cap: gain after the compressor, 0 - 12 dB (0 dB). On ANALOG it drives the valve make-up amp.
RATIO
2:1, 4:1 or 10:1 (4:1). The opto cell on ANALOG is a little softer than the ratio says.
ATTACK
0.3, 1, 3, 10 or 30 ms (10 ms). On ANALOG the opto cell is slower and faster on bigger peaks; on DIGITAL the low band is twice as slow and the high band twice as fast.
RELEASE
0.1, 0.3, 0.6 or 1.2 s, or AUTO (AUTO): programme dependent, quick after short peaks and slower when it works for longer. On ANALOG the opto cell's memory is always there: the longer it compresses, the slower it lets go.
SC HPF
ANALOG only: the side-chain high-pass, on yellow keys (the house colour for a high-pass): OFF, 60, 90 or 150 Hz (OFF). The compressor then reacts less to the bass. Unlit on DIGITAL.
XOVER
DIGITAL only: the crossovers of the 3-band compressor, 120 Hz / 2 kHz, 200 Hz / 3 kHz or 300 Hz / 5 kHz (200 Hz / 3 kHz). The bands sum back flat. The change fades out and back in. Unlit on ANALOG.
ANALOG / DIGITAL
On ANALOG an opto bus compressor: RMS sensing, a wide knee, slow and gluey, followed by a valve make-up amp. On DIGITAL a 3-band compressor (Linkwitz-Riley crossovers), each band its own stereo-linked compressor with a 6 dB knee.

STEREO

The IN / OUT keys in the title strip switch the section in or out.

WIDTH
The side signal, 0 - 200% (100%): MONO at 0%, wider above 100%.
MONO BASS
Yellow keys: OFF, 60, 100 or 150 Hz (OFF). Below it the sides are taken out, so the low end is mono. The change fades out and back in.

LIMITER

The IN / OUT keys in the title strip switch the limiter (and DRIVE) in or out. The title reads VALVE + IRON on ANALOG and BRICKWALL on DIGITAL; the LCD shows the ceiling and the small print the latency in samples.

GR ladder
The limiter's gain reduction (on ANALOG including what the clipper takes off).
DRIVE
A fader with a yellow cap: gain into the limiter, 0 - 12 dB (0 dB).
CEILING
The most it lets out, -12 to 0 dBFS (-0.3 dBFS), as a sample peak.
RELEASE
10 - 1000 ms (100 ms). Programme dependent: it slows when the limiter works for a while.
ANALOG / DIGITAL
On ANALOG the output iron thickens the lows, a gain rider (no lookahead) holds the level and a valve clipper at 2x rounds the peaks it lets through, the negative half a little earlier (even harmonics). On DIGITAL a look-ahead brickwall limiter: the gain is down before the peak arrives (2 ms lookahead) and lets go smoothly; nothing passes the ceiling.

WORD LENGTH

DIGITAL only: the last stage, after OUTPUT. The IN / OUT keys in the title strip switch it in or out; the LCD shows the word length and the shaping. On ANALOG it is out of the path and its keys are unlit.

BITS
16 or 20 bits (16): the word length of the master, 16 for CD.
NOISE SHAPING
OFF (plain TPDF dither), LIGHT or STRONG (LIGHT). The dither noise is moved out of the band where the ear is most sensitive (2 - 5 kHz) towards the very top: LIGHT gently, STRONG with a steep psychoacoustic curve that makes 16-bit fades and tails quieter still. The curves are designed for 44.1 and 48 kHz. Changing BITS or the shaping fades out and back in.

OUTPUT

OUT ladder
The level going out.
LUFS
The short-term loudness (the last 3 seconds, K-weighted) on an LCD.
OUTPUT
A fader: the output level after the limiter and before the word length, -24 to +12 dB (0 dB). Above 0 dB it lifts the peaks over the CEILING.
VALVE
ANALOG only (its label greys on DIGITAL), 0 - 100% (40%): how hard the valves and the iron work, in the EQ, the make-up amp and the limiter.
DRIFT
The series' drift, 0 - 100% (50% is the standard amount): on ANALOG the valves warm up and the mains wander, so the compressor's threshold, the valves' drive and the two channels' gain drift slowly; on DIGITAL only a trace of threshold wander remains.
SAT
The house output stage's band saturation (off by default). Its soft CLIP is always on; the red LED lights when it catches a peak. On DIGITAL with the limiter in, the clip is lifted clear of the CEILING, so the limiter has the last word.

Two models

Every Series 92 unit has two models, chosen with the ANALOG / DIGITAL keys in the header - the two halves of a serious 90s home studio. ANALOG is the boutique outboard: valve, opto and transformer colour. DIGITAL is the first affordable 16 / 20-bit converters and DSP: clean, precise and a little glassy. The two are level matched; switching fades the sound out and back in. The panel lamps glow amber on ANALOG and green on DIGITAL.

ANALOG
A valve mastering chain: a valve program EQ with broad, sweet curves, an opto bus compressor with a valve make-up amp, and a valve-and-iron soft limiter at 2x. VALVE sets the warmth; the word length is out of the path.
DIGITAL
The 90s digital mastering processor: clean EQ, a 3-band compressor, a look-ahead brickwall limiter with a dBFS ceiling, and the word length: 16 or 20 bits with TPDF dither and OFF, LIGHT or STRONG noise shaping. VALVE is out of the path.

Presets

A preset sets the EQ, the compressor, STEREO, the limiter, the word length, OUTPUT, VALVE and the model; TRIM, GUARD, DRIFT and the output stage are left as they are. The presets are levelled to play at a similar loudness, so their OUTPUT sits below 0 dB: raise it (or DRIVE) to bring the peaks up to the ceiling.

Init
ANALOG, 4:1 at -10 dB, auto release, a ceiling of -0.3 dBFS.
Valve Glue
ANALOG, 2:1 with a slow attack and the 60 Hz side-chain filter: the opto as bus glue.
Warm Album
ANALOG, a lift top and bottom, a touch of width and mono bass, the valves a little warmer.
Opto Ballad
ANALOG, gentle 2:1 and a long limiter release for slow, open songs.
Demo Polish
ANALOG, presence and air for a home demo, the 90 Hz side-chain filter, 3 dB into the limiter.
Britpop Loud
ANALOG, faster compression and 6 dB into the valve limiter: dense guitar records.
Valve Low End
ANALOG with the valves and iron pushed hard and the bass mono below 100 Hz.
Clean CD Master
DIGITAL, a gentle 3-band 2:1, a little air, 16 bits with LIGHT shaping.
Club Promo CD
DIGITAL, low crossovers, a fast attack and 6 dB into the brickwall at -0.1 dBFS.
Multiband Pop
DIGITAL, 4:1 across the three bands, 4 dB into the limiter.
Breakbeat Master
DIGITAL, high crossovers and a quick release: the drums keep their punch.
Quiet Acoustic CD
DIGITAL, gentle levelling and STRONG noise shaping for quiet tails.
Reset all to defaults
(Last in the list.) Every control back to its starting value.

Tips

  • Switch between ANALOG and DIGITAL to hear the valve chain against the 90s digital box: at the default settings the level stays the same.
  • On DIGITAL, watch the three GR ladders: if only LO moves, the bass is driving the compression; try a higher XOVER.
  • Leave WORD LENGTH in at 16 bits as the last thing on a CD master; use STRONG shaping for quiet, acoustic material.
  • Keep OUTPUT at 0 dB or below after the limiter: above it, the peaks go over the CEILING.
