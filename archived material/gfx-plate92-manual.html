Modulation & space

Plate92

Series 92's plate: a real steel plate driven by a valve amplifier, or the lush, swirling plate programs of a 90s DSP reverb, on one switch.

Overview

Plate92 is the plate reverb of Series 92: high-end home recording from the 90s, the rack outboard and DSP boxes of a serious home studio of the decade. It has two models on one switch.

ANALOG is a real steel plate in the live room, fed by a valve drive amplifier: a dense, bright-but-smooth build, a damper to set the decay, valve DRIVE on the send and an output transformer that warms the return. DIGITAL is the "plate" programs of the 90s DSP reverbs: a full-bandwidth tank chorused by two LFOs (MOD), a crisp 20-bit top, and three programs on keys - BRIGHT, VOCAL and DRUM - with the program's name on the LCD.

The plate is fed in mono, as a plate is, and the two pickups give the stereo. The models and the programs are level matched, and the return holds its level across the decay, so they change the space, not the loudness.

Reach for it for

  • A classic plate on vocals and snare
  • The lush, chorused vocal plate of 90s records
  • A short, dense plate for drums
  • Long, swirling tails on pads, strings and ballads

Getting started

  1. Insert Plate92 on a track, or on an FX send with MIX at 100%. It opens on Init: ANALOG, 2 s.
  2. Set the decay with DAMPER, and add PRE-DELAY to separate the reverb from the source.
  3. On ANALOG, set the valve's DRIVE on the send; on DIGITAL, pick BRIGHT, VOCAL or DRUM and set the chorus with MOD.
  4. Shape the return with LOW CUT and HIGH CUT, and set its spread with WIDTH.
  5. Set the balance with the MIX fader and the level with the OUTPUT fader.

INPUT

IN / OUT ladders
LED ladders, -30 to +6 (0 = 0 VU): the level coming in and the level going out.
TRIM
Trim before everything, -24 to +12 dB (0 dB).
GUARD
The house input guard: eases very hot sources down (on by default). Its LED lights while it works.

STEEL PLATE / PLATE PROGRAM

The section follows the model. Its LCDs show the plate (ANALOG) or the program's number and name (DIGITAL), and the decay time.

DAMPER / DECAY
The decay time, 0.7 - 5 s (2 s): the damper's position on ANALOG, the program's decay on DIGITAL. The return holds its level as the decay changes.
PRE-DELAY
A delay before the plate, 0 - 250 ms (20 ms).
WIDTH
The pickups' spacing, 0 - 100% (80%): 0 is mono, 100% the full width of the plate.
PROGRAM
DIGITAL only: BRIGHT, VOCAL or DRUM (VOCAL). BRIGHT is a small, very bright plate with the least swirl; VOCAL the lush vocal plate, a larger tank with the most chorus and a silky top; DRUM a short, dense plate that builds at once, with a little mid push. The change fades the return out, clears the plate and fades it back in. The keys are unlit on ANALOG.

VALVE · TONE / DSP · TONE

The title follows the model; the control the model leaves out of the path is greyed.

DRIVE
ANALOG only: the valve drive amplifier on the send, 0 - 100% (30%): soft, slightly lopsided saturation, and the output transformer's lows thicken with it. The level is held as DRIVE rises. Greyed on DIGITAL.
MOD
DIGITAL only: the chorus of the tank, 0 - 100% (40%) - depth and rate together, from a still tail to a deep swirl. How much each program moves is part of its voicing. Greyed on ANALOG.
LOW CUT
A yellow cap (the house high-pass colour): a 12 dB / oct high-pass on the return, 20 - 400 Hz (100 Hz).
HIGH CUT
A black cap (the house low-pass colour): a 12 dB / oct low-pass on the return, 2 - 20 kHz (14 kHz).

OUTPUT

DRIFT
The series' drift, 0 - 100% (50% is the standard amount): on ANALOG the plate's tension and the pickups' gain wander slowly as the room warms and the mains wander, so the tail breathes; on DIGITAL only a trace remains.
SAT
The house output stage's band saturation (off by default). Its soft CLIP is always on; the red LED lights when it catches a peak.
MIX
A fader with a blue cap, 0 - 100% (25%), with a send law: the dry stays at full up to 50% while the reverb comes up, then the dry fades out; 100% is the reverb alone.
OUTPUT
A fader: the output level, -24 to +12 dB (0 dB).

Two models

Every Series 92 unit has two models, chosen with the ANALOG / DIGITAL keys in the header - the two halves of a serious 90s home studio. ANALOG is the boutique outboard: valve, opto and transformer colour. DIGITAL is the first affordable 16 / 20-bit converters and DSP: clean, precise and a little glassy. The two are level matched; switching fades the sound out and back in. The panel lamps glow amber on ANALOG and green on DIGITAL.

ANALOG
A real steel plate in the live room, fed by a valve drive amplifier: a dense, bright-but-smooth build, the pickups' presence and an output transformer that warms the lows. MOD and the PROGRAM keys are out of the path.
DIGITAL
The plate programs of a 90s DSP reverb: a full-bandwidth tank chorused by two LFOs, a crisp 20-bit top (TPDF dither), and BRIGHT, VOCAL and DRUM programs. DRIVE is out of the path.

Presets

A preset sets DAMPER, PRE-DELAY, PROGRAM, DRIVE, MOD, LOW CUT, HIGH CUT, WIDTH, MIX, OUTPUT and the model; TRIM, GUARD, DRIFT and the output stage are left as they are. The presets are levelled to play at a similar loudness.

Tips

  • Switch between ANALOG and DIGITAL on the same settings to hear steel against silicon: the level stays the same.
  • On an FX send, set MIX to 100%: the send law then gives the reverb alone.
  • For the classic 90s vocal plate, take DIGITAL VOCAL, MOD around 60%, 60 - 80 ms of PRE-DELAY and LOW CUT at 150 Hz.
  • On ANALOG, DRIVE does more than distort: the transformer's lows thicken with it, so a little drive fattens a thin snare's plate.
