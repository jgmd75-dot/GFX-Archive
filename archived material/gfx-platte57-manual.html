Platte57

Series 57's plate: the first steel plate of 1957 - one driver, a valve drive amplifier, the damper on its screw, and one pickup or two.

Overview

1957 is the year the plate reverb arrived: a large sheet of steel hung in a frame, a moving-coil driver at one point, a contact pickup at another, and a porous damping plate on a screw that brings the decay down from about five seconds to under one. Platte57 is that first plate, with its valve drive amplifier and the choice of the original single pickup (MONO) or two pickups (STEREO), as the later plates had.

Where Platte81 models the plate's modes, Platte57 is built as a figure-eight tank of diffusers and delays: the dense, bright, instant build of a plate, with no slap.

Reach for it for

  • The classic plate on vocals and snare
  • Bright, dense tails that stay out of the way
  • A mono 1950s plate on period productions
  • Long, shimmering plates on pads

Getting started

  1. Insert Platte57 on a track, or on a send with MIX at 100%. It opens on Init: a 2.2 s stereo plate.
  2. Set the decay with DAMPER: open for long, closed for short. The panel shows the damper moving and the decay time.
  3. Add PRE-DELAY to keep the source clear; thin the lows with LOW CUT.
  4. TENSION makes the plate brighter and denser (tight) or darker (slack).
  5. Try MONO for the original single-pickup plate.

THE PLATE

The plate
The steel in its frame, the driver, the pickup (or two) and the damper; the steel shimmers with the return.
MONO / STEREO
One pickup (the original 1957 plate) or two.
Decay
The read-out under the plate: the decay set by the damper.

DAMPER · TONE

DAMPER
The damping plate's position, OPEN (about 5.5 s) to CLOSED (0.7 s) (45%).
PRE-DELAY
0 - 250 ms (20 ms): a tape machine before the send, as the studios used.
TENSION
Slack (darker, a little slower to build) to tight (brighter, denser) (50%).
LOW CUT
The return's high-pass, 20 - 400 Hz (120 Hz): plates were usually thinned in the lows.
HIGH
A top shelf on the return, -6 to +6 dB (0).

IN · OUT

INPUT
Trim before everything, -24 to +12 dB (0 dB).
GUARD
The house input guard (on by default). Its LED lights while it works.
DRIVE
The valve drive amplifier, 0 - 100% (20%). Oversampled 2x.
WIDTH
With STEREO, mono to wide, 0 - 100% (80%).
MIX
Dry to plate, 0 - 100% (25%). The dry stays at full level up to 50%.
OUTPUT
The output level, -24 to +12 dB (0 dB).
DRIFT
The series' drift, 0 - 100% (50% is the standard amount): the plate's tension wanders, so the tail breathes a little.
SAT
The house output stage's band saturation (off by default). As a mix tool it passes straight through otherwise.

The signal path

The line along the foot: IN, DRIVE AMP, PLATE, OUT.

Two faces

Every Series 57 unit has two faces, chosen with the ULM / COPENHAGEN keys in the header. Each changes the look and the sound, and the two are level matched; switching fades the sound out and back in. Presets leave the face as it is.

ULM
A tight, bright steel plate.
COPENHAGEN
A softer, darker plate; the damper's felt is thicker, so the same position gives a slightly shorter decay.

Presets

A preset sets DAMPER, PRE-DELAY, TENSION, LOW CUT, HIGH, DRIVE, PICKUPS, WIDTH and MIX; the face, INPUT, GUARD, OUTPUT, DRIFT and the output stage are left as they are. The presets are levelled to play at a similar loudness.

Tips

  • A plate builds instantly: it suits sources that need a tail without a room.
  • Thin the lows with LOW CUT so the plate does not cloud the mix.
  • MONO puts the plate behind the source, as on the records of 1957.
  • COPENHAGEN's darker plate sits well behind vocals.
