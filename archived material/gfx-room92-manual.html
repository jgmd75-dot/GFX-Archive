Modulation & space

Room92

Series 92's ambience processor: a real small room miked through a valve pre, or the ambience programs of a 90s DSP box, on one switch.

Overview

Room92 is the ambience unit of the Series 92 home studio: it puts close-miked overdubs into a believable space. Both models build their early reflections from the same geometry: a box room with the room mic and the source in front of it. The dry signal is the direct sound, and each of sixteen reflections off the walls, the floor and the ceiling arrives as much later as its path is longer. SHAPE picks the room, SIZE scales it and DISTANCE moves the source away from the mic.

ANALOG is a real small room miked through a valve mic pre. No real room is a perfect box, so each reflection arrives a little early or late, a little louder or softer and a little off its place in the stereo picture, and every bounce off the soft furnishings of a home studio takes some of the top away: the second bounces more, the carpeted floor more than the walls. A short, natural tail follows, and the valve pre gives the returns a little warmth. DIGITAL is the ambience program of a 90s DSP box: the same reflections, exact and slightly idealised, then a short, dense tail, a defined top and a clean 20-bit return.

The dry signal is never processed. The two models are level matched, and the room's level follows DISTANCE rather than SIZE, SHAPE or DECAY. There is no latency.

Reach for it for

  • Putting close-miked vocals and overdubs into a believable room
  • Matching a dry home-studio overdub to a track recorded in a real room
  • A short, natural room on acoustic guitar, drums and amps
  • Clean 90s DSP ambience programs that leave the source in front

Getting started

  1. Insert Room92 on a track. It opens on Init: ANALOG, a small room, the source a little way off.
  2. Pick the room with the BOOTH, SMALL, LIVE ROOM and CLUB keys, and set SIZE.
  3. Turn DISTANCE up to move the source further from the mic: more room, less direct sound.
  4. Set DECAY for the tail and DAMP for how much top the room takes away.
  5. Switch to DIGITAL for the DSP program, then balance with the MIX fader and set the level with the OUTPUT fader.

Controls

The header

PRESETS

Preset display

ANALOG / DIGITAL
The model: the character of the unit (see Two models). Presets may set it.
S / M / L
Panel size: small, medium or large. The panel also scales to fit the plugin window.
?
Opens a pop-up with the design credit, the GFX website address and the address of this manual.

INPUT

IN / OUT ladders
LED ladders, -30 to +6 (0 = 0 VU): the level coming in and the level going out.
TRIM
Trim before everything, -24 to +12 dB (0 dB).
GUARD
The house input guard: eases very hot sources down (on by default). Its LED lights while it works.

ROOM

The LCD shows the room's dimensions and the source's distance from the mic (ANALOG), or the program and the decay (DIGITAL).

BOOTH / SMALL / LIVE ROOM / CLUB
The room: its proportions, how hard its walls, floor and ceiling are, and the size of its tail. BOOTH is a small, treated vocal booth; SMALL the spare room; LIVE ROOM a larger, livelier room; CLUB a low-ceilinged club room (SMALL by default). Changing it fades the room out and back in; the dry signal carries on.
SIZE
Scales the room, 0.6x to 1.4x, 0 - 100% (50%). The reflections glide to their new places.
DISTANCE
The source's distance from the mic, 0 - 100% (35%). Close: the direct sound leads and the reflections come late and low. Far: the reflections close up on the direct sound and the room and its tail rise (the room's level runs from -11 to +3 dB).
DECAY
The tail, 0.1 - 2 s (0.5 s, RT60). The tail's loudness stays about the same.

WALLS · VALVE / DSP PROGRAM

The section's title follows the model; both controls work on both.

DAMP
A black cap (the house low-pass colour): how much top the room absorbs, 0 - 100% (40%). On ANALOG the soft furnishings: each bounce loses some top, the second bounces and the floor more, and the tail is damped with them. On DIGITAL the program's HF damping on the reflections and the tail (0 = full bandwidth).
WIDTH
The reflections' spread and the tail's side signal, mono to wide, 0 - 100% (80%).

OUTPUT

DRIFT
The series' drift, 0 - 100% (50% is the standard amount): on ANALOG the valve pre's bias wanders as it warms and the mains move, and the air in the room moves the tail a little; on DIGITAL only a trace remains (the box's clock is steady).
SAT
The house output stage's band saturation (off by default). Its soft CLIP is always on; the red LED lights when it catches a peak.
MIX
A fader with a blue cap, 0 - 100% (50%), on a send law: the dry signal stays at full up to 50% while the room comes up, then the room stays at full while the dry signal goes down. At 50% both are at full; at 100% only the room is returned, for use on a send.
OUTPUT
A fader: the output level, -24 to +12 dB (0 dB).

Two models

Every Series 92 unit has two models, chosen with the ANALOG / DIGITAL keys in the header - the two halves of a serious 90s home studio. ANALOG is the boutique outboard: valve, opto and transformer colour. DIGITAL is the first affordable 16 / 20-bit converters and DSP: clean, precise and a little glassy. The two are level matched; switching fades the sound out and back in. The panel lamps glow amber on ANALOG and green on DIGITAL.

ANALOG
A real small room miked through a valve mic pre: irregular early reflections, each bounce softened by the furnishings, a short natural tail and a little valve warmth on the returns.
DIGITAL
The ambience programs of a 90s DSP box: exact, slightly idealised early reflections, a short dense tail, a defined top and a clean 20-bit return with a 17 kHz band limit.

Presets

A preset sets SHAPE, SIZE, DISTANCE, DECAY, DAMP, WIDTH, MIX, OUTPUT and the model; TRIM, GUARD, DRIFT and the output stage are left as they are. The presets are levelled to play at a similar loudness.

Tips

  • DISTANCE is the main control for how much room you hear: it changes the level of the room and the gap between the direct sound and the first reflections together, as moving the mic back would.
  • Switch between ANALOG and DIGITAL on the same room to hear a real room against a DSP program: the reflections land in the same places and the level stays the same.
  • For an overdub that has to match a track cut in a real room, start from SMALL or LIVE ROOM with a short DECAY and raise DAMP until the room stops drawing attention to itself.
  • With MIX at 100% Room92 returns only the room, for use on a send.
