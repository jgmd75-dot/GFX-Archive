Rum57

Series 57's chorus: the organ scanner vibrato - a sixteen-section LC delay line and a motor-driven scanner, V1 - V3 and C1 - C3, in stereo.

Overview

Rum57 is the Series 57 counterpart of Raum81. Before bucket brigades, the chorus of the valve age was the organ's scanner vibrato: a sixteen-section LC delay line, tapped at every section, and a motor-driven capacitive scanner sweeping round the taps.

The sweep is not perfectly smooth (the rotor passes from plate to plate), and the later taps are darker (the signal has passed more coils), which gives the sound its shimmer. V1, V2 and V3 are vibrato (the delayed signal alone); C1, C2 and C3 are chorus (mixed with the direct signal). Rum57 runs two scanners, the right one behind the left, for a stereo spread, through a valve pre-amp oversampled 2x.

Reach for it for

  • Organ chorus and vibrato on keys of any kind
  • A shimmering, old-fashioned chorus on guitars and pads
  • Stereo width with movement
  • Pitch vibrato on vocals and leads (V modes)

Getting started

  1. Insert Rum57 on a track. It opens on Init: C3 at the organs' 6.9 Hz.
  2. Pick a mode: V1 - V3 for vibrato, C1 - C3 for chorus; the higher the number, the deeper.
  3. Set WIDTH for the stereo spread and TONE for how dark the line is.
  4. Slow RATE right down for a slow swirl, or push it up for a fast shimmer.

SCANNER

The IN button next to the title switches the line in or out (the pre-amp stays in).

The scanner
Sixteen plates round the rotor, lit as far as the mode sweeps. The rotor is shown eight times slower than it turns, so the eye can follow it.

VIBRATO · CHORUS

V1 / V2 / V3
Vibrato: the scanner sweeps a third, two thirds or all of the line, and only the delayed signal is heard.
C1 / C2 / C3
Chorus: the same sweeps, mixed with the direct signal. Changing the mode fades out and back in.
RATE
The motor, 2 - 10 Hz (6.86 Hz, as on the organs).
WIDTH
The right scanner runs behind the left, up to half a turn, 0 - 100% (50%). 0 is mono.
TONE
The coils, dark (-) to bright (+) (0).
DRIVE
The valve pre-amp before the line, 0 - 100% (20%). Oversampled 2x.
MIX
Blends the effect with the input, 0 - 100% (100%). The input is delayed to match.

IN · OUT

INPUT
Trim before everything, -24 to +12 dB (0 dB).
GUARD
The house input guard: eases very hot sources down (on by default). Its LED lights while it works.
OUTPUT
The output level, -24 to +12 dB (0 dB).
DRIFT
The series' drift, 0 - 100% (50% is the standard amount): the motor's speed wanders and the two scanners slip against each other.
SAT
The house output stage's band saturation (off by default). Its soft CLIP is always on; the red LED lights when it catches a peak.

The signal path

The line along the foot: IN, PRE-AMP, LC LINE and SCANNER, OUT.

Two faces

Every Series 57 unit has two faces, chosen with the ULM / COPENHAGEN keys in the header. Each changes the look and the sound, and the two are level matched; switching fades the sound out and back in. Presets leave the face as it is.

ULM
A brighter line and a crisper scanner: you hear it step from plate to plate.
COPENHAGEN
Darker coils, a smoother scanner (wider plates) and a rounder pre-amp.

Presets

A preset sets the mode, RATE, WIDTH, TONE, DRIVE and MIX, and switches the line in; the face, INPUT, GUARD, OUTPUT, DRIFT and the output stage are left as they are. The presets are levelled to play at a similar loudness.

Tips

  • C3 is the organ chorus everyone knows; on a guitar or an electric piano it is just as lovely.
  • V modes change the pitch: use them on single lines rather than whole mixes.
  • WIDTH at 100% puts the two scanners in opposition: a big, swirling stereo image that still folds to mono as a chorus.
  • COPENHAGEN's smoother scanner suits pads; ULM's crisp steps suit organs and guitars.
