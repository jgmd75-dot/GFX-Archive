Dynamics

STR57

Series 57's vocal strip: two faces, ULM and COPENHAGEN, voicing a full vocal chain - valve preamp, low end, opto, de-esser, FET, four-band EQ, saturation, artificial double tracking and a ducking spring.

Overview

STR57 is the Series 57 vocal strip: a complete vocal channel in one unit, the sibling of STR92 with the same core chain, voiced for the valve age. After the dynamics, the EQ and the saturation come the two effects of its era: ADT, artificial double tracking on a second tape machine, and SPRING, a two-spring reverb behind a valve send amplifier.

Two models live in the one unit: the series' two faces, picked with the ULM and COPENHAGEN keys in the header. The face voices every stage and changes the faceplate with it:

  • ULM - German modernist: tight and precise. A tight valve preamp (mostly odd colour, a touch of presence), a firmer, quicker opto, precise EQ curves (the Q as set, firm shelves), the valve make-up stage for SATURATION and bright, dense springs. Alabaster, charcoal and signal orange.
  • COPENHAGEN - Danish modern: warm and soft. A warm valve preamp (more even harmonics, the transformer saturating first), a softer, slower opto that leans in, broad EQ curves (wide bells, soft shelves, a sweet, eased top), the output iron for SATURATION and darker springs that drip more gently. Grey birch, sage and oatmeal, with teak cheeks.

A face change ducks the output for 12 ms, swaps at the silent point and fades back in over 12 ms, so it is click-free while the music plays. At Init the two faces are level matched. STR57 runs on mono or stereo tracks, its dynamics are stereo linked, and it has no latency.

Reach for it for

  • A lead vocal with a period, valve-desk character on one plug-in
  • A double-tracked lead without singing it twice (ADT)
  • A close, precise vocal with bright springs (ULM)
  • A warm, rounded croon with dark springs (COPENHAGEN)
  • A spring that stays out of the way while the singer sings (DUCK)

Getting it sounding

  1. Insert STR57 on a vocal track. It opens on Init: ULM, with the spring in.
  2. Set TRIM so the voice sits around -18 dBFS average; GUARD catches anything too hot.
  3. Try ULM and COPENHAGEN on the same take: they are level matched at Init, so it is a fair comparison.
  4. Set the levelling: PEAK REDUCTION on the OPTO for the overall evenness, then AMOUNT on the FET to catch what gets past.
  5. Tame the esses with the DE-ESS (LISTEN helps find the FREQ), shape the tone with the EQ, then add SATURATION to taste.
  6. Set the SPRING MIX and DUCK; switch in the ADT for a double. Or start from a preset.

The chain

The signal runs PREAMP, LOW END, then the dynamics (OPTO, DE-ESS, FET) and the EQ in the order the ORDER key sets, then SATURATION, ADT, SPRING and OUTPUT. On the panel the modules run in the same order: the top row left to right, then the bottom row. The opto always comes before the FET: the slow levelling first, the fast peak catching last. Every IN key fades its stage in or out over 10 ms, and the knobs glide, so nothing clicks.

PREAMP

A transformer and valve front end. The transformer comes first: the lows saturate before the rest. Then a biased, soft-knee valve stage on the house drive standard (an anti-aliased clipper with a pre-filter and a knee that follow the drive). The make-up follows DRIVE, so turning it up adds colour rather than loudness.

IN / OUT
The preamp in or out (in by default).
DRIVE
0 - 100% (30%). ULM (TIGHT VALVE): less bias and a lighter transformer, tighter, with a touch of presence at 3 kHz that grows with DRIVE (up to +0.6 dB). COPENHAGEN (WARM VALVE): more bias and more transformer, thicker, with a little low-mid weight at 220 Hz instead (up to +1.2 dB).
TRIM
The house input stage: -24 to +12 dB (0 dB), before everything else.
GUARD
On by default: gently eases very hot sources down and catches fast peaks so they never overdrive the strip; the lamp lights while it works. It never boosts quiet sources. TRIM and GUARD sit here but are not switched by the preamp's IN key.

LOW END

The house trick for vocals: a gentle high-pass that never goes above 80 Hz, then a low shelf that pulls the 150 Hz region down a little, rather than a high high-pass that thins the voice. Always in.

HIGH-PASS
20 - 80 Hz (60 Hz), 12 dB per octave; fully left (20) reads OFF. A yellow cap: the house colour for a high-pass.
SHELF
A low shelf at 150 Hz, 0 to -3 dB (-2 dB).

OPTO

Slow optical levelling, always before the FET. Program dependent, with a smooth two-stage release: a fast part for the first few decibels and a slow part that takes over after long, loud passages. The make-up is automatic, so PEAK REDUCTION changes the amount of levelling far more than the loudness.

IN / OUT
The opto in or out (in by default).
PEAK REDUCTION
0 - 100% (35%): how far the threshold comes down. ULM holds it firmer: 2.5 : 1 with an 8 dB knee, the threshold from -4 to -34 dB, 8 ms attack, releases of 60 ms and 1.5 s. COPENHAGEN leans on it more: 3.5 : 1 with a 12 dB knee, -4 to -40 dB, 10 ms attack, releases of 80 ms and 2.5 s.
GAIN
-10 to +10 dB (0 dB): output gain on top of the automatic make-up.
GR
The gain reduction meter.

DE-ESS

The new GFX de-esser. It listens for sibilance, not brightness: the cut starts at FREQ and is full an octave above (a steep split just above FREQ), so the presence below is left alone. It only cuts while that band stands out from the rest of the signal, so steady bright material - cymbals, reverb, a full mix through the strip - is left alone while "s" sounds get the full RANGE. Stereo linked. Its split stays in the path when it is switched out, so switching it never combs.

IN / OUT
The de-esser in or out (in by default).
Lamp
Lights while it takes more than 1 dB off.
FREQ
4 - 10 kHz (4.5 kHz): where the cut starts; it is full an octave above. A red cap: the house colour for the highs.
THRESH
-50 to 0 dB (-28 dB): how loud the band must be before it is turned down (it must also stand out from the rest of the signal).
RANGE
0 - 12 dB (6 dB): the most it will take off.
LISTEN
Plays only what the detector hears, so you can tune FREQ to the esses. Switch it off again before you mix.
Readout
The reduction now, or OUT.

FET

A fast peak catcher, always last of the dynamics. AMOUNT drives the voice into a fixed threshold, and the make-up is automatic, so more AMOUNT means more catching rather than more level. Its gentle colour, mostly second harmonic, grows with the gain it takes away.

IN / OUT
The FET in or out (in by default).
AMOUNT
0 - 24 dB (6 dB): the drive into the threshold.
RATIO
4, 8, 12 or 20 (4).
ATTACK
20 - 800 µs (250 µs).
RELEASE
50 - 1100 ms (120 ms).
GR
The gain reduction meter.

EQ

A four-band parametric, LF, LMF, HMF and HF, each column in its band's colour. The model voices the curves, and the line beside the name says how. ULM (PRECISE · FIRM SHELVES · TIGHT Q): the Q as set, firm shelves and narrower LF and HF bells. COPENHAGEN (BROAD · SOFT SHELVES · SWEET TOP): broader bells, soft shelves and a gentler HF shelf, its boost eased and its corner rounded, so the top is sweet and never harsh.

IN / OUT
The EQ in or out (in by default).
LF
GAIN ±15 dB (0 dB), FREQ 30 - 450 Hz (100 Hz), SHELF or BELL (shelf).
LMF
GAIN ±15 dB (+2.5 dB), FREQ 120 Hz - 2 kHz (240 Hz), Q 0.4 - 4 (0.8).
HMF
GAIN ±15 dB (-2 dB), FREQ 600 Hz - 8 kHz (2.8 kHz), Q 0.4 - 4 (0.8).
HF
GAIN ±15 dB (-4 dB), FREQ 1.5 - 16 kHz (7 kHz), SHELF or BELL (shelf).
SHELF / BELL
A change glides the band out over 20 ms, swaps the shape where both are flat and glides it back in.

Init's EQ is a gentle vocal curve: a little low-mid body, a soft scoop at 2.8 kHz and the top eased. Set the GAINs to 0 for a flat start.

SATURATION

AMOUNT
0 - 100% (25%); 0 switches it out (the panel reads OUT). Level matched across its range. ULM (VALVE MAKE-UP): a biased soft knee with a little low-mid weight at 180 Hz. COPENHAGEN (OUTPUT IRON): a soft, symmetric curve with a slight low bump at 90 Hz and a gentle roll-off of the top, both growing with AMOUNT. The valve beside the knob glows with it.

ADT

Artificial double tracking, built on the tape echo of DRP57. The voice is recorded through the valve record amp onto a second machine and played back TIME later, while an oscillator swings that machine's varispeed: a second take, slightly late and slightly off pitch. The record level is fixed per face (ULM 25%, COPENHAGEN 35%), and the transport's 6.3 Hz flutter is there too. The second machine always runs, so a double switched in replays what was already sung. Turning TIME glides the machine, so it is heard as a small pitch bend, as on tape. The reels turn while the double runs.

IN / OUT
The ADT in or out (out by default). MIX and the IN key leave the loudness alone.
TIME
4 - 60 ms (24 ms): how far behind the voice the double runs.
DEPTH
0 - 100% (30%): how far the varispeed swings the time (up to ±30%).
RATE
0.1 - 4 Hz (0.5 Hz): the varispeed oscillator, a sine.
SPREAD
0 - 100% (40%): pans the voice one way and the double the other (centre = both in the middle).
MIX
0 - 100% (70%): the double's level; 100% is as loud as the voice.
SPEED
7 1/2, 15 or 30 IPS (15): the replay character - the head bump (55, 80 or 120 Hz) and the top (6.5, 10.5 or 15 kHz; COPENHAGEN's 0.8 times that, its bump fuller), with a 40 Hz low cut. A change fades the double out for 10 ms, changes the replay and fades it back.

SPRING

The spring of VRB57: two springs (33.1 and 39.7 ms loops), each with a 16-stage dispersive chain in its loop for the drip, behind the valve send amplifier. ULM's springs are bright and dense, COPENHAGEN's darker; the window shows the two springs trembling with the return. Switched out, its tail rings out.

IN / OUT
The spring in or out (in by default).
TENSION
0 - 100% (50%): how much the springs drip.
DECAY
0.5 - 5 s (2 s).
TONE
0 - 100% (50%): the loop's low-pass, ULM 3.1 - 8.1 kHz (5 kHz at 50%), COPENHAGEN 2.3 - 6.2 kHz (3.8 kHz). A black cap: the house colour for a low-pass.
DRIVE
0 - 100% (20%): the valve send amplifier.
MIX
0 - 100% (20%): at 100% the return is about as loud as the voice, whatever the DECAY.
DUCK
0 - 100% (40%), up to 15 dB: the spring pulls back while the voice sings and blooms in the gaps. The voice is followed with a 12 ms attack and a 350 ms release; the full amount is reached when the voice is above about -24 dBFS, none below about -42 dBFS.
DUCK meter
How far the spring is ducked now.

The ADT and the spring can run together in either face.

OUTPUT

DRIFT
0 - 100% (50%, the standard amount; 0 = none). See DRIFT below.
OUTPUT
-12 to +12 dB (0 dB).
IN / OUT
The input and output meters.
CLIP
The house output stage's soft clip, always on (a character unit, as the channel strips and STR92), with its ceiling at -0.3 dB. The lamp lights red while it works.

DRIFT

The series' own drift, 0 - 100%. The valves' bias wanders: the heat of the preamp and the saturation moves, as on an uneven supply, and the two sides never quite match. The ADT machine's capstan wanders (its time breathes, up to ±1.2%, as on DRP57) and its oscillator's rate a little; the springs' hangers move a hair (as on VRB57). At 0 it all holds still.

Presets

Twelve presets, Init and eleven more across both faces. A preset starts from Init and sets the face, the order and every stage, with its OUTPUT set so it plays at about the loudness of Init; TRIM, GUARD and DRIFT stay as they are. The knobs glide to their new values, and a face or order change ducks and fades back in as usual.

Ulm Close Lead
A 70 Hz HPF, a lighter opto, the FET at 8 dB with a faster attack, presence at 3.5 kHz and a 9 kHz lift, and a short, quiet spring with more duck.
Ulm Double-Tracked Lead

Ulm Spring Ballad
More opto, a low bell at 150 Hz, a cut at 400 Hz, presence at 3 kHz and a long, tense spring (3.2 s) high in the mix.
Ulm Radio Voice
A narrow, forward voice: more drive and saturation, HPF 80 Hz, the lows and low mids cut, +4 dB at 3 kHz, the top rolled off, the FET at 10 dB / 8 : 1; the spring out.
Ulm Bright Twist
A bright, quick double: the FET at 10 dB / 12 : 1, presence and an 11 kHz air shelf, a 12 ms ADT at 30 IPS and a tight, bright spring.

Tips

  • Let the OPTO do the levelling and the FET only the catching: a few dB on each sounds more natural than a lot on one.
  • Keep the HPF low and use the 150 Hz SHELF: the voice keeps its chest while the boom goes.
  • Use LISTEN to find the esses, then lower THRESH until the lamp flickers on them only.
  • For a classic double, keep the ADT TIME between 15 and 30 ms with a little DEPTH; for a wide one, raise SPREAD rather than MIX.
  • DUCK lets you run more SPRING than you would dare otherwise: the voice stays dry and clear, and the drip fills the gaps.
  • Compare with REAPER's bypass and set OUTPUT so the loudness matches before you judge.
