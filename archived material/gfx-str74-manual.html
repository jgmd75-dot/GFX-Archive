Dynamics

STR74

Series 74's vocal strip: two models, OB and CLEAN, voicing a full vocal chain - preamp, low end, opto, de-esser, FET, four-band EQ, saturation, a phaser and a ducking tape echo.

Overview

STR74 is the Series 74 vocal strip: the STR92 core chain voiced as the desk of an outside-broadcast truck, with the series' own effects after it: a PHASER and a TAPE ECHO. The echo is the strip's space; there is no reverb.

Two models on one switch, as on every Series 74 unit, picked with the OB and CLEAN keys in the header. The model voices every stage and changes the faceplate with it:

  • OB - the truck: transformer coupled and ballsy. The input iron saturates the lows first, then a single-ended class-A stage (one side of the wave gives before the other); an EQ coupled by transformers; an opto that leans in; the FET's colour; iron and class-A saturation; the phaser's 90 voicing with a little grit; a worn field tape echo. Olive drab modules, amber lamps.
  • CLEAN - the light-grey broadcast desk: an active balanced line driver with lots of headroom (linear until it is pushed hard, then a symmetrical round-off); exact EQ curves; a lighter opto; a cleaner FET; IC-rail saturation; the phaser's smooth OPTO voicing; a well-aligned studio tape echo. Light-grey modules, red-lit keys.

A model change ducks the output for 12 ms, swaps at the silent point and fades back in over 12 ms, so it is click-free while the music plays. At Init the two models are level matched. STR74 runs on mono or stereo tracks, its dynamics are stereo linked, and it has no latency.

Reach for it for

  • A lead vocal with a 70s broadcast-desk character on one plug-in
  • Interviews, spoken word and voice-over (CLEAN)
  • Gritty rock and soul vocals with a tape echo (OB)
  • A slow phase on a vocal or a backing stack
  • An echo that stays out of the way while the singer sings (DUCK)

Getting it sounding

  1. Insert STR74 on a vocal track. It opens on Init: OB, with the tape echo in.
  2. Set TRIM so the voice sits around -18 dBFS average; GUARD catches anything too hot.
  3. Try OB and CLEAN on the same take: they are level matched at Init, so it is a fair comparison.
  4. Set the levelling: PEAK REDUCTION on the OPTO for the overall evenness, then AMOUNT on the FET to catch what gets past.
  5. Tame the esses with the DE-ESS (LISTEN helps find the FREQ), shape the tone with the EQ, then add SATURATION to taste.
  6. Set the TAPE ECHO MIX and DUCK; switch in the PHASER if the song wants it. Or start from a preset.

The chain

The signal runs PREAMP, LOW END, then the dynamics (OPTO, DE-ESS, FET) and the EQ in the order the ORDER key sets, then SATURATION, PHASER, TAPE ECHO and OUTPUT. On the panel the modules run in the same order: the top row left to right, then the bottom row. The opto always comes before the FET: the slow levelling first, the fast peak catching last. Every IN key fades its stage in or out over 10 ms, and the knobs glide, so nothing clicks.

PREAMP

OB: the input iron saturates the lows first, then a biased, single-ended class-A stage. CLEAN: a balanced line driver with lots of headroom and only a trace of iron, rounding off symmetrically when pushed. Both on the house drive standard (an anti-aliased clipper with a pre-filter and a knee that follow the drive). The make-up follows DRIVE, so turning it up adds colour rather than loudness.

IN / OUT
The preamp in or out (in by default).
DRIVE
0 - 100% (30%). OB (IRON · CLASS A): the iron's low weight at 140 Hz grows with DRIVE (up to +1.4 dB). CLEAN (LINE DRIVER): flat.
TRIM
The house input stage: -24 to +12 dB (0 dB), before everything else.
GUARD
On by default: gently eases very hot sources down and catches fast peaks so they never overdrive the strip; the lamp lights while it works. It never boosts quiet sources. TRIM and GUARD sit here but are not switched by the preamp's IN key.

LOW END

The house trick for vocals: a gentle high-pass that never goes above 80 Hz, then a low shelf that pulls the 150 Hz region down a little, rather than a high high-pass that thins the voice. Always in.

HIGH-PASS
20 - 80 Hz (60 Hz), 12 dB per octave; fully left (20) reads OFF. A yellow cap: the house colour for a high-pass.
SHELF
A low shelf at 150 Hz, 0 to -3 dB (-2 dB).

OPTO

Slow optical levelling, always before the FET. Program dependent, with a smooth two-stage release: a fast part for the first few decibels and a slow part that takes over after long, loud passages. The make-up is automatic, so PEAK REDUCTION changes the amount of levelling far more than the loudness.

IN / OUT
The opto in or out (in by default).
PEAK REDUCTION
0 - 100% (35%): how far the threshold comes down. OB leans on it more: 3.5 : 1 with a 12 dB knee, the threshold from -4 to -40 dB, 10 ms attack, releases of 80 ms and 2.5 s. CLEAN is lighter: 2.5 : 1 with an 8 dB knee, -4 to -34 dB, 8 ms attack, releases of 60 ms and 1.5 s.
GAIN
-10 to +10 dB (0 dB): output gain on top of the automatic make-up.
GR
The gain reduction meter.

DE-ESS

The new GFX de-esser. It listens for sibilance, not brightness: the cut starts at FREQ and is full an octave above (a steep split just above FREQ), so the presence below is left alone. It only cuts while that band stands out from the rest of the signal, so steady bright material - cymbals, reverb, a full mix through the strip - is left alone while "s" sounds get the full RANGE. Stereo linked. Its split stays in the path when it is switched out, so switching it never combs.

IN / OUT
The de-esser in or out (in by default).
Lamp
Lights while it takes more than 1 dB off.
FREQ
4 - 10 kHz (4.5 kHz): where the cut starts; it is full an octave above. A red cap: the house colour for the highs.
THRESH
-50 to 0 dB (-28 dB): how loud the band must be before it is turned down (it must also stand out from the rest of the signal).
RANGE
0 - 12 dB (6 dB): the most it will take off.
LISTEN
Plays only what the detector hears, so you can tune FREQ to the esses. Switch it off again before you mix.
Readout
The reduction now, or OUT.

FET

A fast peak catcher, always last of the dynamics. AMOUNT drives the voice into a fixed threshold, and the make-up is automatic, so more AMOUNT means more catching rather than more level. Its gentle colour, mostly second harmonic, grows with the gain it takes away (OB more, CLEAN less).

IN / OUT
The FET in or out (in by default).
AMOUNT
0 - 24 dB (6 dB): the drive into the threshold.
RATIO
4, 8, 12 or 20 (4).
ATTACK
20 - 800 µs (250 µs).
RELEASE
50 - 1100 ms (120 ms).
GR
The gain reduction meter.

EQ

A four-band parametric, LF, LMF, HMF and HF, each column in its band's colour. The model voices the curves, and the line beside the name says how. OB (BROADCAST · PROPORTIONAL Q): coupled by transformers - the bells narrow as they are pushed, the low shelf has a little bump at its corner, the high shelf is soft, and neighbouring bands load each other (a boosted LMF pulls the low shelf's corner up and eats a little of its gain; a boosted HMF pulls the high shelf's corner down). CLEAN (EXACT CURVES): the curves as set.

IN / OUT
The EQ in or out (in by default).
LF
GAIN ±15 dB (0 dB), FREQ 30 - 450 Hz (100 Hz), SHELF or BELL (shelf).
LMF
GAIN ±15 dB (-2 dB), FREQ 120 Hz - 2 kHz (300 Hz), Q 0.4 - 4 (0.8).
HMF
GAIN ±15 dB (+2 dB), FREQ 600 Hz - 8 kHz (3 kHz), Q 0.4 - 4 (0.8).
HF
GAIN ±15 dB (-1.5 dB), FREQ 1.5 - 16 kHz (10 kHz), SHELF or BELL (shelf).
SHELF / BELL
A change glides the band out over 20 ms, swaps the shape where both are flat and glides it back in.

Init's EQ is a gentle broadcast vocal curve: a little cut at 300 Hz, a lift at 3 kHz and the top eased. Set the GAINs to 0 for a flat start.

SATURATION

AMOUNT
0 - 100% (25%); 0 switches it out (the panel reads OUT). Level matched across its range. OB (IRON · CLASS A): a biased knee, one side giving first, the lows thickening at 100 Hz and the top softening, both growing with AMOUNT. CLEAN (IC RAILS): a symmetrical, flat round-off.

PHASER

The GFX Phaser's engine: four all-pass stages with feedback through a soft limiter. OB runs its 90 voicing: 120 Hz - 2.4 kHz, a triangle sweep, with a little grit on the phased path only (the voice itself is never clipped). CLEAN runs its OPTO voicing: 80 Hz - 2 kHz, a wider sine sweep through the photocell's lag (8 ms up, 120 ms down), smooth. The loudness is held as MIX and the IN key move. The display shows the voicing and the sweep's frequency now.

IN / OUT
The phaser in or out (out by default). Switched in, it starts from rest, with no sweep-in.
RATE
0.02 - 5 Hz (0.25 Hz).
CENTRE
0 - 100% (50%): where the sweep sits in the voicing's range (50%: about 540 Hz on OB, 400 Hz on CLEAN).
DEPTH
0 - 100% (60%): how wide the sweep goes.
FEEDBACK
-90 to +90% (+20%), soft-limited in the loop.
MIX
0 - 100% (40%).
STEREO
On (the default), the two sides sweep a quarter turn apart; off, together.

TAPE ECHO

The echo engine of Echofish, its tape type: a cubic tape read, tape saturation (anti-aliased) in the loop, wow and flutter, and the right side a touch longer than the left. OB is a worn field machine (more wow and flutter, a 150 Hz low cut, the tape at 6.5 kHz, a hotter record level); CLEAN a well-aligned studio machine (a trace of wow, a 100 Hz low cut, the tape at 9 kHz). Turning TIME glides the tape, so the repeats bend in pitch. When it is switched out, the feed fades and the repeats ring out.

IN / OUT
The echo in or out (in by default).
Display
FIELD TAPE or STUDIO TAPE and the delay time now (with SYNC when synced).
TIME
60 - 1000 ms (320 ms). Dimmed while SYNC is on.
TONE
0 - 100% (40%): the loop's high cut, 1.5 - 9 kHz (about 3 kHz at 40%). A black cap.
FEEDBACK
0 - 100% (25%).
MIX
0 - 100% (18%): at 100% the echo is as loud as the voice.
SYNC
FREE (the default, TIME sets it), or 1/16, 1/8 T, 1/8, 1/8 D, 1/4 T, 1/4 or 1/4 D of the project tempo.
DUCK
0 - 100% (50%), up to 15 dB: the echo pulls back while the voice sings and blooms in the gaps. The voice is followed with a 12 ms attack and a 350 ms release; the full amount is reached when the voice is above about -24 dBFS, none below about -42 dBFS.
DUCK meter
How far the echo is ducked now.

The phaser and the echo can run together in either model.

OUTPUT

WANDER
0 - 100% (50%, the standard amount; 0 = none). See WANDER below.
OUTPUT
-12 to +12 dB (0 dB). The desk fader.
IN / OUT
The input and output meters.
CLIP
The house output stage's soft clip, always on (a character unit, as the channel strips and STR92), with its ceiling at -0.3 dB. The lamp lights red while it works.

WANDER

The Series 74 drift, 0 - 100%. On OB the heat of the preamp and the saturation wanders, as on an uneven supply, and the two sides never quite match; the phaser's rate, width and centre wander; the tape's wow and flutter move with it. CLEAN has a crystal clock and regulated rails: only a trace of all of it. At 0 it all holds still.

Presets

A preset starts from Init and sets the model, the order and every stage, with its OUTPUT set so it plays at about the loudness of Init; TRIM, GUARD and WANDER stay as they are. The knobs glide to their new values, and a model or order change ducks and fades back in as usual.

Tips

  • Let the OPTO do the levelling and the FET only the catching: a few dB on each sounds more natural than a lot on one.
  • Keep the HPF low and use the 150 Hz SHELF: the voice keeps its chest while the boom goes.
  • Use LISTEN to find the esses, then lower THRESH until the lamp flickers on them only.
  • OB's EQ bands lean on each other like the transformer-coupled desks: if a boost sounds bigger or smaller than its number, that is why. Switch to CLEAN for exact curves.
  • DUCK lets you run more echo than you would dare otherwise: the voice stays dry and clear, and the repeats fill the gaps.
  • Compare with REAPER's bypass and set OUTPUT so the loudness matches before you judge.
