Dynamics

STR81

Series 81's vocal strip: two models, VFD and MATRIX, voicing a full vocal chain - preamp, low end, opto, de-esser, FET, four-band EQ, saturation, a bucket-brigade chorus and a ducking digital delay.

Overview

STR81 is the Series 81 vocal strip: the STR92 core chain voiced as an 80s desk, with the series' own effects after it: the CHORUS of Raum81 and the digital DELAY of DLY81. The delay is the strip's space; there is no reverb.

Two models live in the one unit, picked with the series' VFD and MATRIX keys in the header. The model voices every stage and changes the look and the label language with it:

  • VFD - the Japanese 80s desk: transistor and IC, tight, bright and precise. A clean IC preamp (symmetric, odd colour), the lows kept out of the drive and a touch of presence in; a lighter, quicker opto; an EQ with proportional Q and a crisp top; IC-rail saturation; the chorus's VFD voicing and the delay's VINTAGE DIGITAL model. A charcoal panel, VFD glow, English labels.
  • MATRIX - the European 80s desk: transformer coupled, warmer and rounder. The iron saturates the lows first into a biased (even) knee; an opto that leans in; a broad, gentle console EQ with a sweet top; transformer saturation with a little low bloom; the chorus's darker MATRIX voicing and the delay's CRYSTAL model. Battleship grey, LED blocks, German labels.

A model change ducks the output for 12 ms, swaps at the silent point and fades back in over 12 ms, so it is click-free while the music plays. At Init the two models are level matched. STR81 runs on mono or stereo tracks, its dynamics are stereo linked, and it has no latency.

Reach for it for

  • A lead vocal with an 80s desk character on one plug-in
  • Bright, precise pop and synth-pop vocals with a chorus (VFD)
  • Warm, rounded leads and spoken word (MATRIX)
  • Wide, mono-safe chorused backing stacks
  • A tempo-synced echo that stays out of the way while the singer sings (DUCK)

Getting it sounding

  1. Insert STR81 on a vocal track. It opens on Init: VFD, with the delay in.
  2. Set TRIM so the voice sits around -18 dBFS average; GUARD catches anything too hot.
  3. Try VFD and MATRIX on the same take: they are level matched at Init, so it is a fair comparison.
  4. Set the levelling: PEAK REDUCTION on the OPTO for the overall evenness, then AMOUNT on the FET to catch what gets past.
  5. Tame the esses with the DE-ESS (LISTEN helps find the FREQ), shape the tone with the EQ, then add SATURATION to taste.
  6. Set the DELAY MIX and DUCK; switch in the CHORUS if the song wants it. Or start from a preset.

The chain

The signal runs PREAMP, LOW END, then the dynamics (OPTO, DE-ESS, FET) and the EQ in the order the ORDER key sets, then SATURATION, CHORUS, DIGITAL DELAY and OUTPUT. On the panel the modules run in the same order: the top row left to right, then the bottom row. The opto always comes before the FET: the slow levelling first, the fast peak catching last. Every IN key fades its stage in or out over 10 ms, and the knobs glide, so nothing clicks.

PREAMP

Both on the house drive standard (an anti-aliased clipper with a pre-filter and a knee that follow the drive). VFD: a clean IC and transistor input, symmetric, the lows kept out of the drive. MATRIX: transformer coupled, the iron saturating the lows first into a biased knee. The make-up follows DRIVE, so turning it up adds colour rather than loudness.

IN / OUT
The preamp in or out (in by default).
DRIVE
0 - 100% (30%). VFD (IC · TRANSISTOR): a touch of presence at 3.2 kHz grows with DRIVE (up to +0.6 dB). MATRIX (ÜBERTRAGER): a little low-mid weight at 200 Hz instead (up to +1.2 dB).
TRIM
The house input stage: -24 to +12 dB (0 dB), before everything else.
GUARD
On by default: gently eases very hot sources down and catches fast peaks so they never overdrive the strip; the lamp lights while it works. It never boosts quiet sources. TRIM and GUARD sit here but are not switched by the preamp's IN key.

LOW END

The house trick for vocals: a gentle high-pass that never goes above 80 Hz, then a low shelf that pulls the 150 Hz region down a little, rather than a high high-pass that thins the voice. Always in.

HIGH-PASS
20 - 80 Hz (60 Hz), 12 dB per octave; fully left (20) reads OFF. A yellow cap: the house colour for a high-pass.
SHELF
A low shelf at 150 Hz, 0 to -3 dB (-2 dB).

OPTO

Slow optical levelling, always before the FET. Program dependent, with a smooth two-stage release: a fast part for the first few decibels and a slow part that takes over after long, loud passages. The make-up is automatic, so PEAK REDUCTION changes the amount of levelling far more than the loudness.

IN / OUT
The opto in or out (in by default).
PEAK REDUCTION
0 - 100% (35%): how far the threshold comes down. VFD is lighter: 2.5 : 1 with an 8 dB knee, the threshold from -4 to -34 dB, 8 ms attack, releases of 60 ms and 1.5 s. MATRIX leans on it more: 3.5 : 1 with a 12 dB knee, -4 to -40 dB, 10 ms attack, releases of 80 ms and 2.5 s.
GAIN
-10 to +10 dB (0 dB): output gain on top of the automatic make-up.
GR
The gain reduction meter.

DE-ESS

The new GFX de-esser. It listens for sibilance, not brightness: the cut starts at FREQ and is full an octave above (a steep split just above FREQ), so the presence below is left alone. It only cuts while that band stands out from the rest of the signal, so steady bright material - cymbals, reverb, a full mix through the strip - is left alone while "s" sounds get the full RANGE. Stereo linked. Its split stays in the path when it is switched out, so switching it never combs.

IN / OUT
The de-esser in or out (in by default).
Lamp
Lights while it takes more than 1 dB off.
FREQ
4 - 10 kHz (4.5 kHz): where the cut starts; it is full an octave above. A red cap: the house colour for the highs.
THRESH
-50 to 0 dB (-28 dB): how loud the band must be before it is turned down (it must also stand out from the rest of the signal).
RANGE
0 - 12 dB (6 dB): the most it will take off.
LISTEN
Plays only what the detector hears, so you can tune FREQ to the esses. Switch it off again before you mix.
Readout
The reduction now, or OUT.

FET

A fast peak catcher, always last of the dynamics. AMOUNT drives the voice into a fixed threshold, and the make-up is automatic, so more AMOUNT means more catching rather than more level. Its gentle colour, mostly second harmonic, grows with the gain it takes away (VFD a little cleaner, MATRIX a little more colour).

IN / OUT
The FET in or out (in by default).
AMOUNT
0 - 24 dB (6 dB): the drive into the threshold.
RATIO
4, 8, 12 or 20 (4).
ATTACK
20 - 800 µs (250 µs).
RELEASE
50 - 1100 ms (120 ms).
GR
The gain reduction meter.

EQ

A four-band parametric, LF, LMF, HMF and HF, each column in its band's colour. The model voices the curves, and the line beside the name says how. VFD (PROPORTIONAL Q · CRISP TOP): the bells narrow the harder they are pushed and the shelves are firmer, for a crisp, precise top. MATRIX (BREIT · PULT · SANFT: broad, console, gentle): broader bells and a softer HF shelf, its boost eased and its corner rounded.

IN / OUT
The EQ in or out (in by default).
LF
GAIN ±15 dB (0 dB), FREQ 30 - 450 Hz (100 Hz), SHELF or BELL (shelf).
LMF
GAIN ±15 dB (+1.5 dB), FREQ 120 Hz - 2 kHz (240 Hz), Q 0.4 - 4 (0.8).
HMF
GAIN ±15 dB (-1.5 dB), FREQ 600 Hz - 8 kHz (2.8 kHz), Q 0.4 - 4 (0.8).
HF
GAIN ±15 dB (0 dB), FREQ 1.5 - 16 kHz (9 kHz), SHELF or BELL (shelf).
SHELF / BELL
A change glides the band out over 20 ms, swaps the shape where both are flat and glides it back in.

Init's EQ is a gentle vocal curve: a little low-mid body and a soft scoop at 2.8 kHz. Set the GAINs to 0 for a flat start.

SATURATION

AMOUNT
0 - 100% (25%); 0 switches it out (the panel reads OUT). Level matched across its range. VFD (IC RAIL): a symmetric knee (odd), with a slight lift at 2.5 kHz. MATRIX (ÜBERTRAGER, the output transformer): a biased knee (even), a little low bloom at 100 Hz and a softer top, both growing with AMOUNT.

CHORUS

The chorus of Raum81: two bucket-brigade lines fed from the mono sum and swept in opposite directions, into its output matrix. The wet signal is nearly all side, so it folds back to mono cleanly (MONO SAFE), and its bass stays dry. VFD runs its VFD voicing (3.2 ms, a rounded triangle, brighter); MATRIX its MATRIX voicing (4.6 ms, darker, a sine with the faint ensemble shimmer). The display shows the voicing, the delay, the rate and the depth now.

IN / OUT
The chorus in or out (out by default).
1 - 4
Its four buttons, from the subtlest to the strongest (2 by default): 1 to 3 sweep at 0.25 Hz and grow deeper (±0.35, 0.65 and 1 ms) and wetter; 4 sweeps at 0.5 Hz, ±1.35 ms.
WIDTH
0 - 100% (100%): the matrix cross-feed, from a plain double to the full wide image.
MIX
0 - 100% (60%).

DIGITAL DELAY

The digital delay of DLY81. VFD runs its Japanese VINTAGE DIGITAL model: the time glides (the repeats bend in pitch when TIME moves, as on the old racks), a gentle dual-phase modulation and the 36 kHz, 16-bit vintage clock. MATRIX runs its European CRYSTAL model: ultra clean, and a new time crossfades in over 30 ms with no bend. The loop's low cut is fixed at 150 Hz. When it is switched out, the feed fades and the repeats ring out.

IN / OUT
The delay in or out (in by default).
Display
The model and the delay time now.
TIME
20 ms - 2 s (320 ms). Dimmed while SYNC is on.
FEEDBACK
0 - 90% (25%).
TONE
1.5 - 16 kHz (6 kHz): the loop's high cut. A black cap.
CROSS
0 - 100% (0%): the cross-feed between the sides; turned up, the repeats ping-pong.
MIX
0 - 100% (15%): at 100% the first repeat is as loud as the voice.
DUCK
0 - 100% (50%), up to 15 dB: the delay pulls back while the voice sings and blooms in the gaps. The voice is followed with a 12 ms attack and a 350 ms release; the full amount is reached when the voice is above about -24 dBFS, none below about -42 dBFS.
SYNC
FREE (the default) or 1/16, 1/8 T, 1/8, 1/8 D, 1/4 T, 1/4, 1/4 D or 1/2 of the project tempo (on MATRIX: FREI, and P for the dotted values).
DUCK meter
How far the delay is ducked now.

The chorus and the delay can run together in either model.

OUTPUT

DRIFT
0 - 100% (50%, the standard amount; 0 = none). See DRIFT below.
OUTPUT
-12 to +12 dB (0 dB).
IN / OUT
The input and output meters.
CLIP
The house output stage's soft clip, always on (a character unit, as the channel strips and STR92), with its ceiling at -0.3 dB. The lamp lights red while it works.

DRIFT

The series' own drift, 0 - 100%. The heat of the preamp and the saturation wanders, as on an uneven supply, and the two sides never quite match. The chorus's bucket-brigade clocks and its LFO wander as on Raum81 (the lines ±2.5%, the LFO ±4%), with a trace of BBD hiss while the chorus is in; the delay's clock wanders as on DLY81. At 0 it all holds still.

Presets

Twelve presets, Init and eleven more across both models. A preset starts from Init and sets the model, the order and every stage, with its OUTPUT set so it plays at about the loudness of Init; TRIM, GUARD and DRIFT stay as they are. The knobs glide to their new values, and a model or order change ducks and fades back in as usual.

Init
VFD: DRIVE 30%, HPF 60 Hz, the shelf at -2 dB, the opto at 35%, the de-esser, the FET at 6 dB, the gentle EQ curve, saturation at 25% and the delay in (320 ms, MIX 15%); the chorus out.
VFD Clean Pop Lead
A lighter opto, the FET at 8 dB, a cut at 350 Hz, presence at 3.8 kHz and an 11 kHz air shelf, and a quiet eighth-note delay.
VFD Chorus Verse
The chorus in (button 2) and a 380 ms delay with a little cross-feed, a light lift in the mids and the top.
VFD Quarter Echo
A quarter-note delay with more feedback and cross-feed, ducked hard, with presence and air.
VFD Bright Ballad
A low bell at 170 Hz, strong presence and air, the FET at 9 dB / 8 : 1, a subtle chorus (button 1) and a dotted-quarter delay.
VFD Synth-Pop Double
More drive and saturation, the FET at 10 dB / 8 : 1, the strongest chorus (button 4) and an eighth-note delay with heavy cross-feed.
Matrix Warm Lead

Tips

  • Let the OPTO do the levelling and the FET only the catching: a few dB on each sounds more natural than a lot on one.
  • Keep the HPF low and use the 150 Hz SHELF: the voice keeps its chest while the boom goes.
  • Use LISTEN to find the esses, then lower THRESH until the lamp flickers on them only.
  • The chorus is mono safe: widen a backing stack with it and the mix still folds down cleanly.
  • CROSS on the delay moves the repeats across the stereo field without moving the voice.
  • Compare with REAPER's bypass and set OUTPUT so the loudness matches before you judge.
