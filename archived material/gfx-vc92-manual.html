Modulation & space

VC92

Series 92's studio vocoder: a warm 16-band analogue rack with a sub-octave carrier, or a precise 32-band DSP vocoder, on one switch.

Overview

VC92 is the vocoder of Series 92: high-end home recording from the 90s. A bank of band-pass filters analyses the voice, a follower on each band tracks its level, and the same bank, fed with the carrier, is opened band by band to match.

ANALOG is the boutique analogue rack vocoder of the decade's revival: an input transformer, 16 warm, overlapping bands (the carrier's bank never quite matches the voice's), a rectifier and a single smoothing pole on each band so a little envelope ripple is left in, unvoiced noise for the consonants and an output transformer with a little grit. Its own carrier is a buzz or saw oscillator with a SUB-octave. DIGITAL is a DSP vocoder of the late 90s: 32 precise bands, the voice's and the carrier's exactly matched, fast, clean, ripple-free followers, a clean sibilance pass that lets the voice's own consonants through, and a 20-bit output.

The voice goes in on channels 1/2. The carrier is INT, played from MIDI (six voices: BUZZ, SAW, NOISE or CHORD), or EXT, anything you route to channels 3/4. FORMANT moves the bands to change the size of the throat while the pitch stays put. The two models, the waves and SUB are level matched.

Reach for it for

  • Synth-pop robot voices and talking synths
  • Thick, warm vocoding with a sub-octave under the voice (ANALOG)
  • Choirs and pads sung by a voice (CHORD, or EXT)
  • Clear, articulate vocoding with a formant shift that keeps the pitch (DIGITAL)

Getting started

  1. Insert VC92 on the vocal track. It opens on Init: ANALOG, the INT carrier on BUZZ with 25% SUB.
  2. Arm the track for MIDI as well (or add a MIDI item) and play a chord while the voice plays: the carrier sings with the voice.
  3. Set ATTACK and RELEASE for how quickly it follows, and EMPHASIS and SIBILANCE for clarity.
  4. Switch to DIGITAL for 32 sharp bands and a cleaner, more articulate sound; move FORMANT to change the size of the throat.
  5. For EXT, route a synth or any sound into channels 3/4 of this track and press EXT.

CARRIER

The LCD shows the carrier (the wave, +SUB when the sub-octave is in, or EXT) and the band count: 16 on ANALOG, 32 on DIGITAL.

INT / EXT
The carrier: INT, the internal oscillator played from MIDI, or EXT, whatever is on channels 3/4 of the track (a stereo carrier keeps its stereo). The change fades out and back in.
MIDI
Lights while notes are sounding. MIDI is passed on to the plug-ins after VC92.
HOLD
Keeps the notes sounding after the keys are up, until the next ones (off by default).
WAVE
The internal carrier: BUZZ (a narrow pulse train, very rich), SAW (a sawtooth), NOISE (white noise; it sounds without notes, for a whisper) or CHORD (each key plays its root, fifth and octave). The change fades out and back in.
TUNE
The carrier's pitch, -12 to +12 semitones (0).
OCTAVE
Down one, 0 or up one octave (0).
GLIDE
How long a new note slides from the last, 0 - 100% (0%).
SUB
ANALOG only: a divider's square wave one octave below each note, 0 - 100% (25%), level matched. Its cap is brown, the house colour for the low frequencies. Greyed on DIGITAL, and with the NOISE wave it has no effect.
On EXT
WAVE, TUNE, OCTAVE, GLIDE and SUB are out of use and their labels grey.

FILTER BANK

The section's title gives the band count. The band display shows the level of each band, 100 Hz to 8 kHz, in the lamp colour: amber on ANALOG, green on DIGITAL.

FORMANT
Moves the carrier's bands against the voice's, -12 to +12 semitones (0): down for a larger throat, up for a smaller one. The carrier's pitch is kept.
ATTACK
How quickly the bands open, 0.5 - 50 ms (4 ms).
RELEASE
How quickly they close, 10 - 500 ms (60 ms): short for crisp words, long for a smoother, sustained sound.
EMPHASIS
Lifts the voice's top before the analysis, 0 - 100% (40%): more articulate and brighter. Its cap is red, the house colour for the high frequencies.
SIBILANCE
The consonants, 0 - 100% (40%). On ANALOG, unvoiced noise: a band of noise opened by the voice's top, and the carrier turned towards noise while the consonants last. On DIGITAL, a clean sibilance pass: an unvoiced detector passes the voice's own consonants straight through and turns the carrier to noise for them.

OUTPUT

VOICE ladder
An LED ladder, -30 to +6 (0 = 0 VU): the level of the voice coming in.
TRIM
Trim on the voice, -24 to +12 dB (0 dB).
SAT
The house output stage's band saturation (off by default). Its soft CLIP is always on; the red LED lights when it catches a peak.
DRIFT
The series' drift, 0 - 100% (50% is the standard amount): on ANALOG the circuit warms up and the mains wander, so the centres of the two banks drift, each band on its own, and the carrier's pitch wanders; on DIGITAL the clock is steady: only a trace of pitch drift remains.
GUARD
The house input guard: eases very hot voices down (on by default). Its LED lights while it works.
MIX
A fader with a blue cap: the voice (0%) to the vocoder (100%) (100%).
OUTPUT
A fader: the output level, -24 to +12 dB (0 dB).

Two models

Every Series 92 unit has two models, chosen with the ANALOG / DIGITAL keys in the header - the two halves of a serious 90s home studio. ANALOG is the boutique outboard: valve, opto and transformer colour. DIGITAL is the first affordable 16 / 20-bit converters and DSP: clean, precise and a little glassy. The two are level matched; switching fades the sound out and back in. The panel lamps glow amber on ANALOG and green on DIGITAL.

ANALOG
The boutique analogue rack vocoder: an input transformer, 16 warm, overlapping bands, a little off between the voice and the carrier and drifting with DRIFT, followers with a little envelope ripple, unvoiced noise for the consonants and an output transformer with a little grit. Its own buzz / saw carrier has a SUB-octave. Thick and warm.
DIGITAL
The DSP vocoder of the late 90s: 32 precise bands exactly matched, fast, clean followers, a clean sibilance pass for the consonants and a 20-bit output. FORMANT keeps the pitch. SUB is out of the path.

Tips

  • The voice should be dry and fairly even: compress it before VC92, and keep its reverb after.
  • If the words are hard to follow, switch to DIGITAL, shorten RELEASE and raise SIBILANCE and EMPHASIS.
  • SUB on ANALOG adds weight without muddying the words: try it with SAW an octave down.
  • NOISE needs no MIDI at all: use it for a whispered double under the dry voice, with MIX below 100%.
