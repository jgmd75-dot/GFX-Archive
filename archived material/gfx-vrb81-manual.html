Modulation & space

VRB81

The 81 series digital reverbs: eight models of the 1980s in two racks, European and Japanese.

Overview

VRB81 holds the 81 series' digital reverbs: eight models in two racks, one for each region. The Japanese rack has four Japanese studio and broadcast reverbs of the 1980s: HALL, a massive modular hall; 3D, a reflection engine built from a three-dimensional room model; FLAGSHIP 40, with up to 40 early reflections; and 12-BIT, the early high-density pioneer. The European rack has four European broadcast reverbs: ROOM SIM, a room simulator with no modulation in the tail; BROADCAST, a warm plate and hall; DIALOGUE, a dense speech reverb; and NON-LIN, a gated and reverse processor.

Each rack has its own panel, its own model keys and its own presets. The two share the settings: the reflection and reverb delays and levels, the reverb time and its high and low multipliers, room size, density, diffusion, the filters, DRIVE, the vintage clock, WIDTH, DRIFT and the input and output. SHAPE (for NON-LIN) belongs to the European rack and BITS to the Japanese rack. Every parameter sits on its own display, as on the hardware remotes.

Reach for it for

  • Big, dense halls for pads, strings and ballads (HALL)
  • Realistic rooms and stages with strong early reflections (3D, FLAGSHIP 40)
  • Grainy, bright 80s plates and drum rooms (12-BIT)
  • Natural, mono-compatible rooms for voices and acoustic instruments (ROOM SIM)
  • Speech and voice-over that needs space without blur (DIALOGUE)
  • Gated and reverse effects on drums (NON-LIN)

Two regions

VRB81 has one REGION switch, the EU / JPN keys at the top right of the panel. Each region is its own rack, with its own panel, its own four models and its own presets, and each model wears its own faceplate, after the unit it recalls. Switching fades the sound out, changes over at the silent point and fades back in, so it never clicks. Switching starts the other rack clean. Old projects load in the rack they were saved with.

EU
The European rack: a remote-control panel with a display for every parameter, LED meters and a data wheel. ROOM SIM (navy, red figures), BROADCAST (cream, red figures), DIALOGUE (charcoal, pink figures) and NON-LIN (brushed silver, green figures): built for acoustic precision rather than lushness, with no chorusing in the tail and a transformer on the input.
JPN
The Japanese rack: a remote with lamp-key rows, a dot-matrix reflection display and DOWN / UP keys under every display. HALL (black, with green backlit LCDs), 3D (tan, amber figures), FLAGSHIP 40 (slate blue, cyan figures) and 12-BIT (light grey with coloured keys, red figures): dense reflection grids and glassy digital clarity.

The Japanese rack

Working the displays

Every parameter has its own display. Drag up or down on a display (Shift for fine moves), use the mouse wheel over it, or press its DOWN / UP keys (hold for repeat). Double-click a display to reset it to its default. The last display you touched is outlined.

HEADROOM

HEADROOM LEDs
Four lights showing the input level at -30, -20, -10 and 0 dB. The 0 dB light is red.

PROGRAM

A HALL
The big modular hall: an eight-line network with allpasses inside the loop for massive density. DRIFT moves its read taps by a sample or two so the tail never sets, without any chorusing. Sparse, wide early reflections.
B 3D
The broadcast engine: up to 32 reflections from a three-dimensional room model (DENSITY sets how many) into a high-density tail. Runs at 48 kHz when VINTAGE is on.
C FLAGSHIP 40
Up to 40 individual early reflections (DENSITY sets how many and how wide they spread), then a smooth tail.
D 12-BIT
Six combs at a 31.5 kHz clock through 12-bit converters, with a few early reflections. The vintage clock is always on for this model; BITS can raise the converters to 16 or 24 bits. Its tail is darker at the top.
VINTAGE CLOCK
Runs the reverb at the unit's own clock rate (44.1 kHz for HALL and FLAGSHIP 40, 48 kHz for 3D, 31.5 kHz for 12-BIT) through converters with the bit depth set by BITS. Always lit on 12-BIT.
BITS
Steps the converter bit depth: 12, 16 or 24. Used whenever the vintage clock is on.
READ PRESETS
Opens the list of factory presets.
Preset display
Shows the last preset recalled (P1 - P10), or P-- if none.
◄ / ►
The two keys under READ step to the previous or next preset (wrapping round).

The lamp rows

One-touch settings. The lamp lights on the key that matches the current value.

LOW CUT FREQ
Sets LOW CUT to 30, 50, 100 or 200 Hz.
HIGH CUT FREQ
Sets HIGH CUT to 2.5, 5, 10 or 13 kHz.
RT LOW FREQ RANGE
Sets RT LOW to x0.5, x1.0, x1.5 or x2.0.
RT HIGH FREQ RANGE
Sets RT HIGH to x0.25, x0.35, x0.5 or x1.0.

EARLY REFLECTIONS

A dot-matrix picture of the early reflections across 0 - 240 ms: each column of lit dots is a reflection, taller for louder. A faint dotted column marks where the reverb tail starts (PRE DELAY). The grid is empty when E.RFL LEVEL is OFF.

Reverb parameters

REVERB TIME
How long the tail takes to die away at mid frequencies, 0.1 to 20 seconds (2.4 s by default).
PRE DELAY
The delay before the reverb tail starts, 0 to 250 ms (24 ms by default).
REV LEVEL
The level of the reverb tail, -30 to +6 dB; fully down reads OFF.
E.RFL DELAY
The delay before the early reflections start, 0 to 120 ms (8 ms by default).
E.RFL LEVEL
The level of the early reflections, -30 to +6 dB (-6 dB by default); fully down reads OFF.
ROOM SIZE
The size of the space, 10 to 12000 m3 (2000 by default). It scales the reflection pattern and the lengths of the reverb network.
DENSITY
How many early reflections there are (and on FLAGSHIP 40, how widely they spread), 0 to 100%.
DIFFUSION
How quickly the tail smears into a smooth wash, 0 to 100%. Low settings keep more distinct echoes in the onset.
RT HIGH
The decay time above about 5 kHz (3 kHz on 12-BIT) as a multiple of REVERB TIME, x0.1 to x1.5. Below 1 the top end dies away sooner, as in a real room.
RT LOW
The decay time below about 250 Hz as a multiple of REVERB TIME, x0.5 to x2.5. Above 1 the low end rings on longer.
LOW CUT
A high-pass filter on the reverb, 20 to 1000 Hz (80 Hz by default).
HIGH CUT
A low-pass filter on the reverb, 1 to 20 kHz (12 kHz by default).
DRIVE
A clean preamp drive on the reverb input, 0 to 100%: soft saturation before the reverb.
WIDTH
The stereo width of the reverb, 0 to 150%. 0 is mono; above 100 is wider than normal.
DRIFT
How much the reverb wanders over time, 0 to 100% (20% by default). See Drift below.
MIX
The dry / reverb balance, 0 to 100% (30% by default).

Drift

A slow random wander that keeps the tail alive. On HALL it moves the read taps of the network by up to two samples - more density, never a pitch wobble. On the other models it lets the air absorption (the high-frequency damping in the tail) wander. On 12-BIT the old clock also runs very slightly unsteady. The wander speeds up as you turn DRIFT up.

IN / OUT

TRIM
Input level before anything else, -24 to +12 dB. Click the display to select it, then drag, use the mouse wheel or DOWN / UP.
GUARD
Off by default. When on, it gently eases very hot sources down towards about -18 dBFS average and catches fast peaks above -6 dBFS, so loud sources never overdrive the plugin. It never boosts quiet sources.
LEVEL
The output level, -24 to +12 dB.
SAT
Band saturation on the way out, 0 to 100%: only the lows and highs are saturated (the mids stay clean), with a soft curve that loosens as the level rises.

The European rack

Using the displays

Every parameter has its own display. Click a display to hand it to the data wheel (its frame lights up); drag the display up or down (hold Shift for fine steps), use the mouse wheel over it, or press its - and + keys (hold them to repeat). Double-click a display to return it to its default.

Meters

IN / REV
Two LED columns in 3 dB steps: the input level and the reverb's output level.

MODEL

ROOM SIM
A room simulator: the reflections are worked out from the room's shape (first and second order, with a slight difference between the ears), then an 8-line network with no modulation at all - true room modes that hold up in mono.
BROADCAST
The compact broadcast plate / hall: damped parallel combs into allpasses, a warm loop and a heavier input transformer.
DIALOGUE
A dense allpass tank tuned for speech: high diffusion, fast recovery, no flutter.
NON-LIN
A long tapped line with a shaped envelope instead of a natural decay. TIME sets the length of the shape (up to about 1.25 s, stretched further by SIZE).
SHAPE (RM / GT / RV)
NON-LIN only. RM: a falling, room-like envelope. GT: gate - flat, then cut off. RV: reverse - the sound rises, then stops.
VINTAGE
Runs the reverb through the converters of the era, with the clock rate and bit depth of each model (14-bit on BROADCAST, 16-bit on the others; 31.25 to 40 kHz).

1st REFLECTION

DELAY
When the early reflections start, 0 to 120 ms.
LEVEL
The level of the early reflections, -30 to +6 dB; fully down reads OFF.

REVERB

DELAY
The pre-delay before the reverb tail, 0 to 250 ms.
LEVEL
The level of the tail, -30 to +6 dB; fully down reads OFF.

REVERB TIME

HIGH END
A multiplier for the decay time of the highs, 0.1 to 1.5 x. Below 1 the top dies away faster, like real air absorption.
TIME
The decay time at mid frequencies, 0.1 to 20 seconds.
LOW END
A multiplier for the decay time of the lows (below about 250 Hz), 0.5 to 2.5 x.

ROOM

SIZE
The room volume, 10 to 12000 cubic metres. It sets the spacing of the reflections and the size of the reverb network (and the length of the NON-LIN shape).

REFLECTIONS

DENSITY
How many early reflections are used, 0 to 100%. Low settings give a few distinct reflections; high settings a dense cluster.
DIFFUSION
How much the input is smeared before it enters the reverb, 0 to 100%. More diffusion gives a smoother, less grainy onset.

FILTER

LO CUT
A high-pass on the reverb, 20 to 1000 Hz.
HI CUT
A low-pass on the reverb, 1 to 20 kHz (shown in kHz).

CHARACTER

DRIVE
The input transformer, 0 to 100%: the lows saturate first, for weight and warmth. BROADCAST's transformer is heavier.
WIDTH
The stereo width of the reverb, 0 to 150%. 0 is mono; above 100 is wider than natural.
DRIFT
0 to 100%. A slow wander of the air absorption (the high-end damping) - never the delay lengths, so there is no pitch movement. On NON-LIN the tap levels also shift slightly. The wander speeds up as DRIFT goes up.

OUTPUT

MIX
Dry / reverb balance, 0 to 100% (30% by default).

PARAMETERS

Data wheel
Changes whichever display was clicked last (named under SELECTED). Drag it (Shift for fine steps) or use the mouse wheel.
Program display
Shows the preset in use (P1 to P10), or P-- when none is loaded.
LIST
Opens the list of all presets by name. The last entry resets everything to the defaults.
◄ / ►
Step to the previous or next preset.
1 - 9, 0
Memory keys: recall presets 1 to 9, and 0 for preset 10.

IN / OUT

TRIM
Input level before anything else, -24 to +12 dB.
GUARD
Off by default. Gently eases very hot sources down towards about -18 dBFS average and catches fast peaks above -6 dBFS. It never boosts quiet sources.
SAT
Band saturation on the way out, 0 to 100%: only the lows and highs are saturated (the mids stay clean), with a soft curve that loosens as the level rises.
LEVEL
Output level, -24 to +12 dB.

Tips

  • Use E.RFL LEVEL and REV LEVEL separately: reflections alone place a sound in a room without washing it out; the tail alone gives space without clutter.
  • A PRE DELAY of 20 - 60 ms keeps a vocal clear of its own reverb.
  • Lower RT HIGH (the x0.35 or x0.5 lamp) for a more natural, darker room; raise RT LOW for warmth.
  • On a send, set MIX to 100% and use LEVEL to set the return level.
  • For a natural room, start in ROOM SIM, set SIZE to the space you imagine, then adjust TIME; it holds up well when the mix is checked in mono.
  • Pull HIGH END below 1 to make a long reverb sound darker and further away without touching HI CUT.
  • On speech, DIALOGUE with a short TIME and LO CUT around 150 - 200 Hz adds space without smearing the words.
